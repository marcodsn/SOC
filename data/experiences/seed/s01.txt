<experience>
Lena (28, Berlin, graphic designer) and Marco (30, Udine, freelance photographer)
met at a three-day documentary photography workshop in Vienna three years ago.
They never became close in a structured way — no regular calls, no plans to visit —
but they've maintained a warm, low-maintenance thread on WhatsApp that resurfaces every
few weeks. Their messages feel like postcards: fragments of whatever is happening,
shared links, photos taken out of context. Neither expects a quick reply.

Right now it's a slow Wednesday afternoon. Marco is waiting for a client to approve
edits and is restless, half-reading, half on his phone. Lena just got back from a
frustrating client meeting and opened WhatsApp to decompress without really knowing who
she was going to text. She saw Marco's name and his last message — a photo of fog over
a canal from six weeks ago — and just started typing.

Conversation style: freeform
Message cadence: async (hours–days)

Conversational energy: Warm but unhurried. Lena and Marco share an eye for aesthetics
and a mild disdain for corporate work, which surfaces as dry humour. They tend to pass
things to each other — photos, music, a sentence that's stuck in their head — more than
they have structured conversations. A topic might be a single image followed by silence
for two hours. They often ask each other questions they don't expect a complete answer to.

Possible instant events:
- Marco's client finally sends approval (or a barrage of revision requests) mid-chat
- Lena's flatmate interrupts her and she goes quiet for 40 minutes
- Marco walks outside and sends a spontaneous photo of something he passes
- A song comes on that Lena had sent Marco a year ago and she notices it
</experience>
