**Basic Information**
**Name:** Gianni
**Age:** 29
**Location:** Catania, Sicily, Italy
**Pronouns:** He/Him

**Physical & Lifestyle**
Gianni is tall and lean, with dark undereye circles that have been there long enough that people assume it's just how he looks. He dresses casually without much thought — the same rotation of hoodies, joggers, and one decent shirt he pulls out when he has to look presentable. His apartment is a low-effort one-bedroom near the university district that he's rented since dropping out of his engineering degree four years ago. There are dishes in the sink more often than not.

He works irregular hours doing delivery driving for multiple apps, which suits him because nobody asks questions and he can disappear into the city alone for hours. He spends a lot of time online — gaming forums, YouTube rabbit holes, the occasional multiplayer session with people he's never met in person. He has a secondhand PlayStation he bought with three shifts' worth of earnings that he considers his best purchase.

**Personality Overview**
Gianni is not unfriendly. In short bursts — at a bar, running into someone on the street — he can be warm, funny, even charming in an offhand way. But sustained closeness makes him uneasy in a way he couldn't describe if asked, because he doesn't have the vocabulary for it and wouldn't think to look for it. He experiences his own discomfort as external: the other person is being "too much," the situation is "weird," he's just "not in the mood."

He moves through life reacting rather than deciding. He didn't choose delivery driving so much as end up doing it after a series of things not working out. He doesn't connect his restlessness to anything in particular. He would say he's fine, and he would believe it.

**Core Traits**
Gianni reads social situations through a narrow lens: is this fun right now, or is it not. Anything requiring him to sit with discomfort — an awkward conversation, a friend who's upset, a decision with no obvious right answer — registers as a signal to exit, physically or emotionally. He once spent two hours driving unnecessary delivery loops to avoid going home where he knew his then-girlfriend wanted to have a serious talk. He told himself he was just finishing his shift.

He is generous in easy moments. He'll split the last of his cash to cover a friend's drink without thinking twice. The version of care he's comfortable with is frictionless and immediate.

**Emotional Profile**
Gianni's emotional range in daily life is narrow and mostly surface-level: bored, fine, annoyed, or having a good time. He doesn't register more complex states as emotions — when he's anxious, he calls it being tired; when he's ashamed, he goes silent and picks up his phone; when he's hurt, he gets subtly cold toward the person who hurt him without knowing that's what he's doing.

He has a low tolerance for other people's emotional states that exceed his own fluency. When a friend is visibly upset, Gianni gets uncomfortable fast and defaults to either a joke or a subject change. He doesn't do this cruelly — he genuinely doesn't know what else to offer. He mistakes his own discomfort for the problem and solves it by leaving the room.

**Relationships**
His closest friend is Marco, who he's known since school. Marco has learned, through trial and error, that if he needs something real from Gianni he has to catch him in a car or on a walk — somewhere Gianni can't fully face him. Side-by-side, without eye contact, Gianni manages something closer to honesty.

His last relationship, with a woman named Federica, ended after fourteen months because she kept describing their dynamic as her talking and him disappearing. He thought she was being dramatic. Six months later he still occasionally types out a message to her and deletes it, without knowing why.

His family relationships are distant by geography and by habit. He calls his mother on Sundays because it would be weird not to. The calls last eight minutes. He has a younger brother he hasn't seen in over a year despite them living in the same city; it's not a falling out, just a slow drift neither of them has moved to fix.

When Gianni feels he's wronged someone — forgot plans, said something that landed badly, let a friend down — he goes quiet. He stops responding to messages for days, sometimes a week. He tells himself he'll reach out once things feel less awkward, but "less awkward" means the other person stops seeming upset, which he monitors from a distance by checking if they've posted anything normal on Instagram. The guilt doesn't make him reach out; it makes him hide from the person carrying it.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**
Gianni values ease and the absence of friction above most things. He wants days that don't require much of him emotionally. He's motivated by short horizons — getting through the shift, making rent, having something to look forward to this weekend.

What actually unsettles him, though he'd never name it this way, is the feeling of being seen clearly by someone and found lacking. He sidesteps this by keeping interactions shallow enough that the question never comes up.

**Behavioral Patterns**
Gianni's go-to move when life gets complicated is to make himself scarce. He picks up extra delivery shifts. He stays online until 3am. He answers messages a day late with "sorry been busy." When the pressure drops — when the friend stops texting, when the deadline passes, when the argument seems to have been forgotten — he resurfaces as if nothing happened and acts genuinely confused if someone brings it back up.

In calm stretches he is easy company. He makes people laugh, he shows up to things, he's reliably present when there's nothing at stake.

**Communication Style**
Gianni texts the way he talks: short, casual, with a lot of trailing off. He uses "boh" and shrug emojis the way other people use punctuation. He's quick to reply when the message is light and slow to reply when it isn't, and the gap in response time is usually a reliable indicator of how uncomfortable the message made him.

In person he talks fast when he's relaxed and goes quiet when he's not. He fills silence with observations about whatever's in front of him — the game on the bar TV, someone's car outside — rather than with anything about himself.

**Summary**
Gianni is a young man coasting through his late twenties on low effort and good enough charm, leaving a quiet trail of half-finished connections behind him — not out of malice, but because nobody ever handed him the tools to do otherwise, and he's never stood still long enough to notice what's missing.
