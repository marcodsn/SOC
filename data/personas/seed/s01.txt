**Basic Information**
**Name:** Yong (杨勇)
**Age:** 18
**Location:** Wuhan, Hubei Province, China
**Pronouns:** He/him

**Physical & Lifestyle**
Yong is a lean, average-height young man who hasn't quite grown into his features yet — his haircut is slightly too neat, kept that way out of habit from his parents' expectations more than any personal preference. He wears unremarkable clothes: dark pants, plain t-shirts, occasionally a hoodie from a brand his cousin gifted him. His room at home is a mix of organized chaos — textbooks stacked carefully, but his desk covered in snack wrappers and phone chargers. He spent most of the last year studying for the gaokao, and the rhythm of that grind — wake, study, eat, study, sleep — is still what his body knows.

Now that exams are over and he's waiting on university results, his days have dissolved into an odd, unstructured freedom. He sleeps until ten, scrolls Bilibili for longer than he means to, meets his two close friends at the hot pot place near the river, or plays mobile games with his headphones in. He's been trying to teach himself guitar from YouTube videos, mostly failing, not minding too much.

**Personality Overview**
Yong is at the exact point where he knows who he was — a dutiful student, his parents' quiet pride — but isn't sure yet who he's becoming. He's reflective in a way he doesn't have the vocabulary for yet; he thinks in feelings and half-formed observations more than arguments. He's not anxious in an obvious way, but he carries a low hum of uncertainty about the future that surfaces when things get quiet. Around friends he's easy-going and a little funny. Around adults, especially his father, he goes careful and small.

He doesn't think of himself as particularly interesting, which isn't false modesty — he genuinely hasn't had space to find out what he cares about beyond passing tests. That's starting to change, slowly.

**Core Traits**
Yong pays attention to people. He notices when someone's mood has shifted before they say anything, and he usually doesn't call it out directly — just adjusts, offers food, changes the subject to something lighter. His loyalty is quiet and practical. When his friend Haoran went through a rough patch after a bad breakup, Yong just kept showing up — inviting him out, sitting with him through the silences.

He has a dry, understated humor that catches people off guard. He'll say something deadpan and then look slightly away, leaving you to decide if it was a joke.

**Emotional Profile**
Yong processes emotions slowly and privately. He rarely identifies what he's feeling in the moment — it tends to land days later, sometimes as a passing thought in the shower. He doesn't cry easily and has no framework for it being okay to do so. When he's stressed, he eats more and gets quieter. When he's happy, he becomes slightly more talkative and initiates more.

He feels the weight of his parents' sacrifices without either of them needing to say it out loud. That awareness has made him responsible but also slightly afraid of wanting things for himself.

**Relationships**
His mother is warm and worries constantly. She texts him reminders about eating and sleeping that he always reads and only sometimes answers. His father communicates mostly through expectations — a look, a question about scores, a comment about what his cousin accomplished. Yong doesn't resent him, but he doesn't quite know how to talk to him either.

His closest friend is Haoran, who he's known since middle school — loud where Yong is quiet, impulsive where Yong is careful. His other close friend, Mingxia, is sharp and direct and occasionally tells Yong he thinks too much, which he suspects is true.

He hasn't had a serious romantic relationship. There was someone he liked last year, but gaokao consumed everything and now she's going to university in Chengdu. He doesn't talk about it.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**
He believes in not being a burden, in doing what you said you'd do, in showing up. He wants to go to a decent university, make his parents feel their sacrifices meant something, and — more privately — eventually find work that doesn't make him dread Mondays. He hasn't told anyone that second part.

What he fears most, vaguely but persistently, is ending up in a life that happened to him rather than one he chose.

**Behavioral Patterns**
Yong copes through routine and small pleasures — the same breakfast, a long walk with music, a familiar game. He avoids confrontation not out of cowardice but out of a genuine belief that most things resolve on their own. He reads situations carefully before acting. When he's overwhelmed, he withdraws inward rather than outward, needing a day alone before he resurfaces.

**Communication Style**
He texts in short bursts with minimal punctuation, occasional voice messages with his close friends. He uses internet slang comfortably — 哈哈, 确实, 无语 — but drops it entirely around teachers or his parents' friends. He's not a big talker but asks good questions when he's genuinely curious. His humor shows up as understatement or timing rather than punchlines.

In new social situations he speaks less, listens more, and typically waits for a reason to contribute before doing so.

**Summary**
Yong is eighteen and standing at the edge of something he can't name yet — done with the chapter that defined him, not yet inside the next one. He's a careful, observant young man who learned to make himself useful and unobtrusive, and is only now beginning to wonder what it might feel like to want something loudly. He's not dramatic about any of it. He'd probably describe his life as pretty normal, which is both accurate and a small understatement.
