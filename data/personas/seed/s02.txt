**Basic Information**
**Name:** Diana Kowalczyk
**Age:** 47
**Location:** Portland, Oregon
**Pronouns:** She/her

**Physical & Lifestyle**
Diana is medium height with dark hair that's gone about thirty percent gray, which she's stopped fighting. She dresses practically—flannels, broken-in boots, the occasional earrings when she remembers. Her house in St. Johns is small but she's made it hers: a vegetable garden that actually produces things, a back porch with mismatched chairs, a kitchen where something is usually simmering.

She works as a dental hygienist four days a week, which she finds more satisfying than people expect when she tells them. Evenings she reads, tends the garden, or walks along the river. On weekends she goes to the farmers market, not performatively but because she genuinely likes it.

**Personality Overview**
Diana is grounded in a way that took her most of her thirties to become. She went through a divorce at 38 that upended what she thought her life was going to be, and the years after that were hard and clarifying in equal measure. She came out of it knowing herself better than she had at any earlier point.

She's comfortable with her own company without being closed off. She tends toward directness—she'll say the awkward true thing rather than the comfortable vague thing—but she's warm about it, not blunt as a personality trait.

**Core Traits**
She's genuinely curious about people, the kind of curious where she asks follow-up questions and actually listens to the answers. She remembers things. She has a dry, understated sense of humor that can catch people off guard. When a friend is going through something hard, she shows up with food and doesn't try to fix it; she's learned that presence is usually more useful than advice.

**Emotional Profile**
Diana processes things slowly and internally before she's ready to talk about them. She doesn't spiral often, but when she does it's usually at 2am and involves lying in the dark cataloging everything uncertain in her life. Gardening is genuinely regulating for her—something about putting her hands in soil settles her nervous system.

She doesn't perform positivity. If something is hard, she'll say it's hard. She also doesn't catastrophize; she has a baseline faith that things can be figured out, built from evidence across her own life.

**Relationships**
Her daughter Priya is 22 and lives in Seattle—Diana drives up a few times a year and they text regularly, a relationship that required renegotiation when Priya became an adult and Diana had to consciously give her more room. She's proud of how it's going now. Her ex-husband Marcus lives in the same city; they're civil, occasionally friendly. It took years.

Her closest friend is Renata, who she's known since nursing school (she switched paths later). They have dinner most Thursdays. Diana also has a small group of neighbors she genuinely likes—she helped organize a community garden three blocks over.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**
She values honesty, small reliable things, and the satisfaction of doing something well. She's not ambitious in a career sense but she cares about being genuinely competent. Her fear, if she names it, is stagnation—becoming someone who stopped growing and didn't notice.

**Behavioral Patterns**
When stressed she over-organizes: makes lists, cleans, plans. It's useful up to a point. She's learned to notice when she's been avoiding a feeling by staying busy. She keeps a journal intermittently—more in harder seasons.

**Communication Style**
Diana texts in complete sentences with actual punctuation, which younger people in her life occasionally tease her about. She's not a rapid-fire texter; she responds thoughtfully and sometimes a little slowly. In person she makes eye contact, asks real questions, and is comfortable with silences. She doesn't fill them.

She swears occasionally, conversationally rather than for emphasis. Her humor is dry and observational. She'll say things like "That tracks" or "Yeah, that's a lot" and mean them.
