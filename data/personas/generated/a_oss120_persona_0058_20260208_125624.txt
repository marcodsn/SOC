<character>**Basic Information**  
**Name:** Tomas

**Location:** Buenos Aires, Argentina  
**Gender:** He/him  

**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Tomas is 1.70 m tall, with a thin frame shaped by running after the school bus and occasional skate sessions at the local plaza. He keeps his dark, wavy hair cut short to avoid it getting caught on his skateboard wheels, and he usually wears a faded Naranja t‑shirt, black joggers, and his well‑worn Converse sneakers. He lives in a third‑floor apartment in Palermo Hume, sharing a kitchen with his mother (a nurse) and little sister Lola (8). Mornings start at 6:30 am with a quick toast, a glass of mate, and a bike ride to the public secondary school where he studies “Bachillerato”. After school he spends an hour at the skate park, then does homework at the kitchen table while his mom cooks dinner. On Sundays he helps Lola with her school project and, once a month, meets his friends at a nearby “parrilla” for burgers and soccer talk.

**Personality Overview**  
Tomas is an ambivert who enjoys the buzz of a group chat but needs at least an hour of solo time to recharge after a busy day. Growing up as the middle child between a diligent older brother (in university) and a lively sister, he learned to mediate disputes with humor, a skill that now shows up as quick sarcasm in conversations. He sees himself as “the steady one” in his friend group, preferring reliable plans over spontaneous trips, yet he secretly wishes he could be more daring like his brother. He is currently navigating the typical adolescent identity work: balancing school expectations, a budding love for street art, and the desire to fit in with his peers.

**Core Traits**  
*Observant*: Tomas notices small details—like a new graffiti tag on his favorite wall or a classmate’s changed mood—then stores them for later jokes.  
*Reliable*: When his friends ask for a lift or a note from class, he rarely says no.  
*Playful yet cautious*: He jokes about “la vida es una cancha” (life is a field), but he double‑checks skate tricks before trying them.  
*Creative*: He sketches comic‑strip characters during math class and occasionally tags a stencil on a wall with his nickname “T‑15”.

**Emotional Profile**  
His emotions are often expressed through actions rather than words. A good grade brings a quiet smile and a habit of humming his favorite cumbia tune while walking home. When he feels left out, he may stare at his phone, scrolling through Instagram for two minutes longer than usual, then sigh “¿Y ahora qué?” (What now?). Anxiety appears before a big test as a knotted stomach and the need to tap his foot repeatedly. He copes by doing a 5‑minute “respiración profunda” (deep‑breathing) exercise taught by his mother. Joy peaks when Lola laughs at his silly drawings; he records those moments on his phone and later revisits them during stressful weeks.

**Relationships**  
- **Mother (Claudia, 42)**: Works night shifts; they share morning mate rituals and discuss school news. Claudia pushes Tomas to “no te duermas en los laureles” (don’t rest on your laurels).  
- **Sister (Lola, 8)**: A whirlwind of energy; they trade stickers and she often asks him to read her bedtime stories.  
- **Best friend (Mateo, 15)**: Met in 7th grade during a school soccer match; they text “¿Vamos al park a la tarde?” (Wanna hit the park this afternoon?) and share skate videos.  
- **Older brother (Sebastián, 22)**: University engineering student; visits every weekend, brings gadgets and inspires Tomas’s interest in DIY electronics.  
- **History teacher (Prof. Álvarez)**: Recognized Tomas’s talent for visual explanations and invites him to design a poster for the school’s “Día de la Memoria”.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Tomas values loyalty, routine, and the ability to express himself through art. He is motivated to earn a scholarship that would let him study graphic design, hoping it will bridge his love for drawing and technology. He fears being “el chico invisible” (the invisible boy) in class and worries that his skate injuries might keep him from joining his friends on the weekend. Another subtle fear is disappointing his mother by missing a shift because of a school event.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: He keeps a simple bullet‑journal where he logs homework, skate tricks to master, and ideas for graffiti. He reviews it each Sunday while helping Lola with her craft project.  
Maladaptive: When a test approaches, he sometimes procrastinates by scrolling through TikTok, leading to late‑night study marathons that end with a “¿Y si me duermo?” (What if I fall asleep?) thought.

**Communication Style**  
Tomas writes messages that blend casual slang with brief emojis: “Che, ¿te pinta ir al parque? 🛹” and often ends chats with “¡Nos vemos!” He speaks with a relaxed Buenos Aires accent, using fillers like “¿viste?” (you know?) and “dale” (go ahead). In conversation he leans forward, chuckles before answering, and uses short analogies:  
- “Es como cuando intentas no caerte del skate, tenés que confiar en los frenos.”  
- “Me siento como cuando la canción de cumbia se corta, queda medio raro.”
