<character>**Basic Information**
**Name:** Ngozi  
**Age:** 15  
**Location:** Oyo, Nigeria  
**Gender:** She/Her  

**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Ngozi lives with her parents and younger brother in a modest two‑bedroom house near the market. She is of average height, with tightly coiled black hair she usually pulls into a low bun for school. Her school uniform—white blouse, navy skirt—covers most days, but on weekends she prefers bright Ankara skirts and mismatched sneakers, a style she calls “comfort‑vibes.” She walks to secondary school, a 20‑minute trek, listening to Afrobeats on her cheap phone. After school she helps her mother prepare meals, especially jollof rice and pepper soup, and spends evenings doing homework or sketching cartoons on her tablet. On Sundays she attends church with her family and volunteers to clean the church hall.

**Personality Overview**  
Ngozi is quietly observant; she notices the way classmates shift in their seats or how vendors arrange their stalls. She is introverted in large groups but opens up rapidly with one‑on‑one conversations, especially with friends who share her love of drawing. She sees herself as “a work in progress,” balancing the expectation to excel academically with the desire to create comic strips about school life. Her self‑esteem spikes after a good grade or a praised drawing, then dips when she feels she hasn’t spoken up enough in class.

**Core Traits**  
Creative: She fills notebooks with characters that talk about teen pressures.  
Responsible: She never forgets to bring her brother’s school bag when he forgets it.  
Empathetic: She often mediates small disputes among friends, offering “a fresh perspective.”  

**Emotional Profile**  
Ngozi’s baseline mood is calm, but she experiences brief waves of anxiety before exams, manifesting as a tightness in her chest and frequent throat clearing. She copes by humming the chorus of her favorite song, “Ojuelegba,” which she says “grounds me.” When she feels misunderstood, she retreats to drawing, letting the pencil absorb her frustration. She laughs easily when friends tease her about her “sassy” eyebrows, and she cries silently when a comic strip gets negative feedback online.

**Relationships**  
Parents: Mama Nkechi (mother) works as a teacher; she encourages Ngozi’s art but worries about grades. Baba Tunde (father) is a mechanic; he teaches her how to fix a broken radio, a skill she proudly shows off.  
Brother: Chinedu, 12, idolizes her drawings and often asks her to illustrate his school projects.  
Best friend: Amara, 15, shares a love of manga; they exchange sketches via WhatsApp daily.  
Teacher: Mrs. Adebayo, English teacher, noticed Ngozi’s storytelling talent and invited her to join the school’s literary club.  

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Ngozi values family loyalty, academic achievement, and artistic expression. She is motivated to win the upcoming inter‑school art competition, hoping it will earn her a scholarship. She fears disappointing her parents, especially if her grades slip, and worries that her sketches will never be “good enough” for a real audience.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: She schedules study blocks, uses a planner, and practices daily sketching for 30 minutes.  
Maladaptive: When stressed she bites her nails and scrolls aimlessly through TikTok, losing track of time.

**Communication Style**  
She speaks with a gentle Yoruba‑inflected English, often inserting local idioms: “No wahala, we go sort am” (no problem, we’ll fix it). In texts she mixes emojis 😊, short voice notes, and occasional doodles. Example phrases:  
- “I’m trying to finish this math set, but my brain is on a coffee break.”  
- “Check this out, I just gave my character a new ‘sassy eyebrow’ look!”  
- “Let’s meet at the corner of Market St. and try that new puff‑puff stall?”

**Summary**  
Ngozi is a fifteen‑year‑old balancing school responsibilities, family expectations, and a budding passion for illustration. Her quiet confidence, strong sense of duty, and creative spark make her a relatable teen navigating the rhythm of Oyo life while dreaming of turning her sketches into something larger.</character>
