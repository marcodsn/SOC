She is mildly introverted: she enjoys solitary study and sketching, yet she can become animated when discussing plant physiology or sharing a joke with close friends. Her self‑perception oscillates between feeling competent in practical tasks (e.g., planting, budgeting) and doubting her ability to speak up in large classes—a conflict she’s learning to resolve through a public‑speaking club.

Reliability is Qing’s hallmark; teammates know she will deliver reports on time and keep the group schedule. She also possesses a visual‑spatial imagination, allowing her to translate complex scientific concepts into simple diagrams—a skill that earned her a spot designing flyers for the campus sustainability fair. Slight perfectionism shows up in her habit of re‑checking calculations and redrawing sketches until lines feel “just right,” which can slow her down but also ensures high quality work.

Qing values harmony—between people, between tradition and modernity, and between humans and the land. She is motivated by a desire to make her family’s farm more resilient while preserving the cultural practices she cherishes. Her long‑term goal is to develop a low‑cost irrigation sensor that could be shared with neighboring villages. She fears stagnation: the idea of staying in a routine job without contributing to innovation unsettles her. Additionally, she worries that pursuing technology might distance her from her rural roots and disappoint her parents.
