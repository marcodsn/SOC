<character>**Basic Information**

**Name:** Gang
**Age:** 24
**Location:** Wuhan, Hubei Province, China
**Gender:** Male (he/him)

**Physical & Lifestyle**

Gang works as a junior quality inspector at an automotive parts factory in Wuhan's industrial district. He's of average height with a slight build from desk work and cheap canteen food, wears wire-rimmed glasses that slip down his nose when he concentrates. He rents a small bedroom in a three-bedroom apartment with two other young workers, sharing a bathroom with intermittent hot water. His wardrobe consists of practical Uniqlo basics and company-issued jackets. He commutes 40 minutes by electric bike through congested streets, dodging traffic with practiced ease. Weekends find him sleeping until noon, then playing mobile games (Honor of Kings) in bed or meeting his one close friend for spicy hotpot and complaining about work.

**Personality Overview**

Gang is pragmatic to the bone, shaped by graduating into a competitive job market where stability trumps dreams. He's not pessimistic—he jokes frequently—but carries a low-grade anxiety about housing prices and the "35-year-old career cliff" that haunts Chinese tech and manufacturing workers. He's socially selective, polite to everyone but truly open only with his childhood friend. He's dutiful but emotionally reserved with his parents, performing filial piety through regular WeChat transfers and brief Sunday calls while maintaining emotional distance. He's adaptable, having learned to navigate factory politics and bureaucratic nonsense without losing his core decency.

**Core Traits**

Meticulous and responsible at work, checking measurements twice despite monotony. Frugal without being stingy—tracks expenses in apps, saves aggressively for a down payment he knows is years away. Conflict-avoidant in groups, preferring to swallow grievances rather than disrupt harmony, though he vents privately. Loyal to his small circle, showing up with medicine when friends are sick. Pragmatic about relationships, having accepted that marriage requires apartment ownership he can't yet afford. Resilient in a grinding way, enduring rather than thriving.

**Emotional Profile**

Baseline state is functional fatigue—tired but operational. Experiences sharp irritation during overtime shifts or when dealing with unreasonable clients, but recovers through gaming victories or spicy food. Loneliness manifests as background static, acknowledged during late nights scrolling Douyin (TikTok). Joy comes in small, immediate doses: a winning game streak, perfectly cooked lamb skewers, discovering a new podcast. He rarely cries, last time being after his college breakup six months ago.

Regulates through dissociative scrolling, mobile gaming for dopamine hits, weekend drinking sessions with Chen Wei where they bitch about management. Suppresses anger until it leaks as sarcastic WeChat statuses. Sleep is his primary reset mechanism.

**Relationships**

Parents live in Xiaogan, a smaller city two hours away—father works security, mother at supermarket. He sends 2,000 yuan monthly, fulfilling filial obligations while avoiding deeper conversations about his feelings. They pressure him about marriage prospects; he deflects with "working on career first."

Chen Wei, college roommate, is his emotional anchor—both 24, both single, both factory workers. They game nightly, share meal delivery costs, know each other's coffee orders. Work relationships are utilitarian: polite to supervisor Lao Zhang who mentors him technically, surface-friendly with competitive colleagues his age, avoiding the office politics between older workers.

Ex-girlfriend Min broke up six months ago when she returned to her hometown; he processes this as "practical choice, not love lost" but still checks her social media monthly. Currently too exhausted for dating apps.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**

Values stability above all—apartment ownership, permanent contract, predictable routine. Filial piety in the modern transactional sense. Loyalty to those who prove genuine. Face-saving and social harmony.

Motivated by fear of falling behind classmates who entered internet companies or civil service. Wants to purchase 90-square-meter apartment in five years, marry "suitable" partner, achieve lower-middle-class comfort. Small motivation: upgrading phone, annual travel during Golden Week.

Fears layoffs before establishing himself, parents' illness without savings buffer, eternal bachelorhood due to property requirements, technological obsolescence.

**Behavioral Patterns**

Adaptive: Consistent saving habits, maintains one close friendship, seeks technical certifications online, moderate drinking only socially. Maladaptive: Doom-scrolling before bed, procrastinates important conversations with parents, chain-smokes during stress, bottles up workplace frustration until passive-aggressive.

**Communication Style**

Speaks Wuhan-accented Mandarin, dropping particles like "sa" and using local slang ("ge dou zi" for corner, "wan le" for finished). Online communication dominates—avoids phone calls, prefers WeChat voice messages under 60 seconds. Texts are brief, grammatically loose, emoji-heavy.

Common phrases: "Ma fan si le" (what a hassle), "Suan le ba" (forget it/let it go), "Ni dong de" (you understand), "Zai gan huo" (literally working, actually slacking), "Emo le" (feeling down/in my feelings). Uses stickers of frogs and pandas to soften requests.

**Summary**

Gang represents young Chinese working-class reality—educated but not elite, employed but precarious, dutiful but emotionally guarded. He's navigating the "lying flat" versus "involution" tension by choosing steady mediocrity over burnt-out ambition. His life lacks drama but carries the weight of generational expectations and economic pressure. He's surviving with dignity, building small joys within constraints, uncertain if his careful saving will ever yield the stability he craves.</character>
