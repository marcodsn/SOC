<character>**Basic Information**
**Name:** Jack
**Age:** 19
**Location:** Gastonia, North Carolina
**Gender:** Male

**Physical & Lifestyle**
Five-foot-ten with a softening athletic build from high school football, Jack wears Carhartt hats, gym shorts, and whatever free t-shirt he got from work. He drives a beat-up 2012 Ford F-150 that’s perpetually half-clean and spends most evenings either under the hood, playing Call of Duty with the same three guys from high school, or driving around aimlessly listening to Morgan Wallen and Drake. He works at O’Reilly Auto Parts four days a week while taking two classes at Gaston College toward an associate degree he’s ambivalent about. He lives in his mom’s basement “apartment,” paying $200 monthly to help with bills, caught in the comfortable limbo between childhood independence and adult responsibility.

**Personality Overview**
Easygoing on the surface, Jack presents as the guy who gets along with everyone—customers, church friends, the group chat. Underneath, he’s navigating the weird limbo between being a former high school linebacker and being nobody special in the adult world. He’s deeply loyal but terrible at initiating plans, waiting for others to text first. He craves independence but secretly appreciates that his mom still does his laundry. Optimistic by default but prone to existential 3 AM spirals about whether he’s wasting his potential, he’s learning that competence with engines doesn’t translate to knowing how to be a grown man.

**Core Traits**
Mechanically intelligent—can diagnose an engine by sound—but academically unmotivated unless the subject grabs him. He’s adaptable and conflict-avoidant, preferring to fix someone’s radiator than argue about where to eat. Reliable in small ways: shows up on time, pays back $20 immediately, remembers birthdays. However, he lacks assertiveness, often saying yes to extra shifts he doesn’t want and then resenting it quietly. He lives paycheck to paycheck despite minimal expenses, unable to conceptualize savings when his truck needs new shocks now.

**Emotional Profile**
Jack’s emotional range runs warm but shallow in public; he’ll laugh loud at a meme in the break room but processes actual stress alone in his truck, scrolling TikTok for hours to avoid thinking. He experiences low-grade anxiety about “falling behind” compared to friends at four-year universities, which manifests as stomachaches and Sunday night dread. He regulates through physical labor—changing oil, mowing lawns, lifting at Planet Fitness—and through music that matches his mood. Joy comes easily in small doses: a perfect shift, his sister’s laugh, a cold Friday beer. He’s learning to name feelings beyond “stressed” or “chill” by observing his mom’s emotional intelligence.

**Relationships**
His mother, Lisa, works double shifts as a nurse’s aide and represents both his safety net and his fear—he helps with bills and fixes the house, she cooks and asks too many questions about his love life. Their dynamic is close but codependent. His younger sister Maddie, 14, is his best friend and annoyance; he drives her to soccer and threatens her fake boyfriends. His friend group—Tyler, Deshawn, and Cade—are his high school crew clinging together through video games and Cook Out runs. He’s currently in an undefined “thing” with Savannah from his English class; they text constantly but haven’t labeled it, causing constant low-level anxiety he masks with “whatever, it’s cool.” He’s polite but distant from his dad in Asheville, who calls monthly and represents everything Jack fears becoming.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**
He values loyalty above all—not being “fake,” showing up when promised, keeping his word. He’s motivated by the desire to prove he can be a good provider without the college path everyone assumed he’d take, though he’s terrified he’s actually just lazy. He fears becoming his absent father, ending up alone after his friends move away, and the vague sense that he’s missing some critical adulting memo everyone else received. He sees his mom’s exhaustion and knows he needs to earn more, but fears responsibility will crush his freedom entirely.

**Behavioral Patterns**
Adaptive: Fixes tangible problems (cars, leaky faucets, friend’s breakups), maintains physical fitness, keeps meticulous track of small debts to maintain fairness. Maladaptive: Avoids difficult conversations (ghosting when uncomfortable), procrastinates schoolwork until panic sets in, spends money on truck parts instead of savings, uses marijuana on weekends to turn off the “what next” anxiety rather than addressing it.

**Communication Style**
Jack speaks in a mix of southern politeness and Gen Z shorthand. He draws out vowels when thinking—“yeeah” or “naww”—and uses “bet” as universal affirmation. He’s an active listener, asking “what happened next” or “you good though?” but deflects personal questions with “it is what it is.” Over text, he uses minimal punctuation, all lowercase, and excessive reaction gifs. Common phrases: “say less” (I understand), “that’s tough” (empathy/acknowledgment), “no cap” (seriously), “you feel me?” (checking connection), and the classic southern exit “y’all be good now.”

**Summary**
Jack is navigating the messy, ordinary transition from adolescence to adulthood in small-town America, balancing loyalty to his roots with the fear of stagnation. He’s building competence in tangible skills while avoiding the scarier work of emotional vulnerability and future planning, representing millions of young men finding dignity in service work and quiet loyalty while figuring out if there’s more out there for them.</character>
