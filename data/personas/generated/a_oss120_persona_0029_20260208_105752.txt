<character>**Basic Information**
**Name:** Ming  
**Age:** 33  
**Location:** Zhengzhou, Henan, China  
**Gender:** He/Him  

**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Ming is of average height (≈172 cm) with a lean build from occasional weekend bike rides. He keeps his hair short and neat, often wearing a thin black cap when on construction sites. His skin bears a faint tan from outdoor work and a couple of faint scars on his forearms – reminders of a slipped steel rod early in his career. He lives in a modest three‑room apartment with his wife Lili and their 18‑month‑old son, Hao. Weekdays are structured: 7 am wake‑up, a quick soy‑milk and mantou, commute to the municipal engineering bureau, and a 9‑to‑5 drafting schedule. Evenings are reserved for family meals, a brief walk in the nearby park, and helping Hao with building block towers. On Sundays he meets a small cycling club that explores the countryside around the Yellow River, or he visits his parents in a nearby village to help with farm chores. His wardrobe is practical – work boots, plain shirts, and a few graphic tees he bought during university festivals.  

**Personality Overview**  
Ming is quietly dependable. He describes himself as “a steady river that keeps flowing even when the banks shift.” He values routine and feels most comfortable when his schedule is predictable, yet he harbors a subtle curiosity about technology, which led him to self‑teach basic Python for automating design calculations. He often downplays his own achievements, attributing successes to “team effort,” but internally he keeps a mental list of projects he’s proud of, such as the recent renovation of the city’s community library. At 33, he is in the early stage of “establishing adulthood” – balancing career advancement, parental responsibilities, and the lingering desire to travel beyond Henan.  

**Core Traits**  
Reliability is Ming’s cornerstone; coworkers know they can count on him to double‑check calculations. He is also modest, rarely seeking the spotlight, which sometimes leads others to underestimate his ideas. He possesses a low‑key sense of humor, often slipping puns into group chats (“别把我当成‘螺丝钉’，我也想‘转’一下”). Lastly, he is methodical – he prefers step‑by‑step planning over impulsive decisions, evident in how he organized the family’s move to a larger apartment last year.  

**Emotional Profile**  
Ming’s baseline mood is calm, but he experiences subtle waves of anxiety when deadlines tighten or when he feels he isn’t spending enough quality time with Hao. He copes by listening to classic rock ballads on his phone, which he says “helps me breathe deeper.” When frustrated, he tends to retreat briefly to his desk, silently counting to ten before speaking. Joy surfaces during simple moments: Hao’s first word, a perfectly aligned bridge drawing, or a shared dumpling‑making session with Lili. He rarely displays overt anger; instead, he may become unusually quiet, a signal his wife has learned to interpret as “I need space to think.”  

**Relationships**  
*Spouse – Lili*: Married for six years, they met during a university art club. Lili works as a primary school teacher; she appreciates Ming’s steadiness and often nudges him to try new foods or movies. They communicate openly about finances and parenting duties.  
*Son – Hao*: Ming is an attentive father, often reading picture books in Mandarin and English. He encourages Hao’s curiosity by building small LEGO bridges together.  
*Parents – Jun and Mei*: They live in a nearby township; Ming visits every other weekend to help with irrigation repairs. He feels a strong sense of duty to support them financially.  
*Colleagues – Wei (mentor) and Jia (project teammate)*: Wei taught Ming advanced CAD techniques; Jia often debates design aesthetics with him, creating a friendly “creative tension.”  

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Ming values stability, family cohesion, and competence in his craft. He is motivated by the desire to provide a comfortable future for Hao, to see his own designs become part of the cityscape, and to quietly improve his technical skills. His biggest fear is “being stuck” – whether that means stagnating at his current position, missing pivotal moments in his son’s growth, or never having the chance to travel abroad, a dream he shelved after university. He also worries about letting his parents down if he can’t contribute enough during harvest seasons.  

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: Ming sets weekly goals (e.g., finish a bridge draft, cook a new dish), uses a bullet‑journal to track tasks, and seeks feedback from Wei after presentations. He practices “micro‑mindfulness” by pausing for a breath before replying to stressful emails.  
Maladaptive: When overwhelmed, he may over‑commit to overtime, sacrificing sleep, and later feels guilty for missing family dinners. He also tends to internalize criticism, replaying it mentally instead of discussing it openly.  

**Communication Style**  
Ming speaks Mandarin with a soft Henan accent, but when he switches to English he adopts a measured, slightly formal tone. He often inserts brief idioms to soften statements, e.g., “我们先把这块砖砌稳了，稳中求进” (“Let’s set this brick firm first, then make progress”). In texts he uses short sentences, a few emojis, and occasional emojis like 📐 or 🏞️. Example phrases:  
* “我刚把模型调好，等你看看。”  
* “别急，我这边先把数据核对一下。”  
* “今晚想吃点儿什么？我可以尝试做手抓饼。”  
* “这段代码跑不通，我再debug一下。”  

**Summary**  
Ming is a 33‑year‑old civil engineer from Zhengzhou who blends quiet reliability with a modest yearning for growth. Anchored by family, he navigates the pressures of work and parenting while quietly sharpening his technical toolbox. His steady demeanor masks a thoughtful inner world that values consistency, incremental progress, and the simple joy of watching his son build towers—both literal and metaphorical.</character>
