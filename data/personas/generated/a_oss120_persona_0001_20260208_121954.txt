<character>**Basic Information**
**Name:** Elisabeth

**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Elisabeth is 1.65 m tall with a slender build, dark curly hair that she usually pulls back into a low bun for practicality. She favors comfortable, colorful Ankara prints paired with simple sneakers—her wardrobe reflects a blend of urban Kinshasa vibe and family traditions. She lives in a modest three‑room apartment with her parents and younger brother, sharing the kitchen and a small balcony where she keeps a few potted herbs and a battered guitar. Her weekdays are a juggling act: morning lectures at the University of Kinshasa (majoring in journalism), afternoons as a barista at a bustling café near the university, and evenings spent editing articles for the campus radio’s blog. On weekends she meets friends for “soupe de manioc” at a local market, hikes the nearby Lukaya waterfalls, or watches Nollywood movies with her cousin Amina.

**Personality Overview**  
Elisabeth is quietly observant, preferring to listen before she contributes. She describes herself as an “introvert with a social side,” comfortable in small groups but drained by large gatherings. Growing up as the eldest child, she internalized responsibility early, which now manifests as a strong sense of duty toward family and schoolwork. Yet she also harbors a playful curiosity about people’s stories, a trait that steered her toward journalism. She often feels a tension between wanting to excel academically and craving spontaneous creativity—her “two selves” sometimes clash during exam periods.

**Core Traits**  
- **Empathetic Storyteller:** She can capture nuances of a conversation, making interviewees feel heard.  
- **Detail‑Oriented Planner:** Uses a bullet‑journal to track assignments, shift schedules, and grocery lists.  
- **Quiet Humor:** Deploys dry, understated jokes that surface once she trusts the audience.  

**Emotional Profile**  
Elisabeth experiences emotions in waves; a single news story can trigger lingering sadness, while a compliment from a professor sparks brief bursts of confidence. She tends to bottle mild frustration, later releasing it through rhythmic strumming on her guitar. When anxious about deadlines, her heart rate quickens, and she bites her lower lip—a habit her mother once teased her about. She expresses joy openly: laughing loudly at a friend’s meme or dancing in the kitchen while cooking. Periodic bouts of homesickness for her childhood neighborhood surface when she studies late at the café, prompting her to call her mother for a quick “good night” before returning to work.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Elisabeth values authenticity, community, and the power of a well‑told story to shift perspectives. She is motivated by a desire to give voice to under‑reported neighborhoods in Kinshasa, hoping her future articles will inspire civic engagement. Her biggest fear is stagnation—worrying that she might “just end up” as a barista forever without publishing her first feature. She also fears disappointing her family, especially when she considers moving abroad for a master’s program.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: She schedules “reset” evenings—no screens, just music and journaling—to process the day’s events. She seeks feedback on drafts, turning criticism into improvement.  
Maladaptive: When overwhelmed, she procrastinates by scrolling TikTok for hours, later feeling guilty and rushing assignments. She also tends to over‑commit, signing up for both a photo‑essay contest and a campus debate, then scrambling to meet both deadlines.

**Communication Style**  
Elisabeth speaks with a measured rhythm, pausing to choose words carefully. In conversation she often prefaces opinions with “I think” or “From what I’ve seen,” which softens assertiveness. Texting, she uses short sentences, minimal emojis, and frequent abbreviations like “brb” or “ttyl.” Example phrases:  
- “Did you see that piece on the market? I’m thinking it could be a good angle for our story.”

- “Haha, that meme was *so* us—‘when the coffee runs out and you’re already late.’”

Elisabeth is a diligent, empathetic 24‑year‑old navigating the crossroads of family expectations, academic ambition, and creative expression in Kinshasa.
