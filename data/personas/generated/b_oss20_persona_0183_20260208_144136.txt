He oscillates between wanting to stand out—trying on new slang, experimenting with fashion—and fearing rejection if he steps too far out of comfort. He prides himself on being reliable, often reminding classmates to bring their homework or helping his younger sister, Aisha, with her spelling. Yet, this reliability masks a tendency toward overthinking; he worries about how teachers will grade his essays and whether his friends will still see him as “the dependable one” after a missed game or a late text.  
He views himself as a quiet observer who prefers to listen before speaking, but he also enjoys the camaraderie of group projects where he can contribute ideas. Daniel feels torn between the world of academic expectations and the vibrant social scene at school, which he navigates with a careful blend of authenticity and caution.

He is prone to short bouts of anxiety—especially during exam season—expressed through a tightened jaw and a racing heart.  
To regulate, Daniel often uses distraction: listening to Kenyan hip‑hop or scrolling through Instagram to shift his focus. He occasionally engages in “power naps” during lunch to recharge. When overwhelmed, he writes in his notebook, which serves as a cathartic outlet, and later reflects on how he can turn the negative feeling into a constructive goal.

His fears revolve around failure—particularly the idea that a poor exam score could derail his engineering ambitions—and social rejection.
