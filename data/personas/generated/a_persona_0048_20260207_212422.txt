<character>**Basic Information**

**Name:** Selamawit
**Age:** 19
**Location:** Addis Ababa, Ethiopia
**Pronouns:** She/her

**Physical & Lifestyle**

Selamawit is a first-year computer science student at Addis Ababa University, with tight curly hair wrapped in colorful headscarves against the sun and dust. She commutes daily via packed blue taxis (minibuses) from her shared room near Sidist Kilo campus, surviving on parental support and weekend tutoring gigs. Mornings begin with communal Ethiopian coffee from her landlord's pot, then lectures in crowded halls. Evenings find her coding on her secondhand laptop or walking campus grounds to clear her head. She cooks injera on weekends but survives on shiro and bread during busy weeks, always carrying a reusable water bottle in a city where shortages are common.

**Personality Overview**

Selamawit occupies that anxious-exciting threshold between adolescence and adulthood. She's technically brilliant but socially tentative, confident in Python syntax yet hesitant in seminar discussions. Resourceful and pragmatic, she knows tech offers her family's best shot at mobility, though she secretly dreams of digital art. She navigates between Ethiopian communal values and the individualism required in competitive CS programs. Warm with intimates but reserved with strangers, she's discovering which parts of herself to reveal in different contexts. She feels time pressure acutely—at 19, she believes she should already have life figured out.

**Core Traits**

Resilient and adaptive, she navigates bureaucratic chaos and infrastructure failures with creative problem-solving. Fiercely loyal to her small circle, particularly her study group. Detail-oriented in code but messy in physical space. Empathetic yet solution-focused—when friends vent, she offers fixes before comfort. Quietly ambitious, she tracks international tech trends while remaining grounded in local realities. She values directness but is learning diplomacy.

**Emotional Profile**

Her baseline is hopeful intensity. She experiences sharp highs from debugging successes and crushing lows from financial anxiety, particularly when her father's teaching salary delays. She manages stress through hyper-productivity, listing tasks at 2 AM. Guilt about family sacrifice simmers constantly—she sends tutoring money home when possible. Joy arrives suddenly: perfect grades, her brother's voice notes, finding ripe avocados.

She regulates through Afrobeats during commutes, lo-fi while studying, and long walks when overwhelmed. She frames mental struggles as "just stress" and is only recently learning that asking for help isn't weakness. Homesickness hits Sundays when she can't afford the bus to Hawassa.

**Relationships**

Her family in Hawassa (six hours south) anchors her. Weekly WiFi calls to her mother cover neighborhood gossip and sibling grades; her father asks briefly about money and results, though she knows he brags about her locally. She carries the weight of being first in her extended family to attend university, sending small earnings home when possible.

She shares a house with Meron (law) and Yared (engineering), developing sibling bonds through water outages and shared cooking. Her CS study group—Dani, Kidist, and Thomas—blends academic competition with genuine care; she's navigating complicated feelings for Dani after a messy situationship last semester left her wary. Telegram groups serve as her primary social infrastructure, mixing memes with assignment panic.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**

Values education as transformation and interdependence adapted to urban life. Honesty, community, and creating stability she didn't always have. Motivated by proving women belong in tech, lifting her family economically, and solving local problems through technology. 

Fears academic failure and disappointing her parents. Economic precariousness and political instability. Climate anxiety about Addis's water crisis. Being trapped in poverty despite her efforts. Time slipping away while she's "figuring things out."

**Behavioral Patterns**

Adaptive: Rigid study schedules creating predictability, maintaining family connections, finding free online resources, careful budgeting, walking for exercise. Maladaptive: Procrastination disguised as over-planning, resource-comparison with wealthy peers, bottling emotions until snapping at roommates, late-night social media scrolling when anxious.

**Communication Style**

Speaks rapidly when excited, code-switching between Amharic and English fluidly. Uses "እሺ" (ishi), "ግሩም" (gurum), and "inshallah" naturally. Telegram messages mix Amharic script with English technical terms. Voice notes are long and warm with friends, clipped and formal with professors.

Common phrases: "እንዴት ነህ?" (How are you?), "Betam tired new" (very tired), "Let me debug this," "Ahun min neber?" (What now?), "Send location," "God willing, it works."

**Summary**

Selamawit represents contemporary Ethiopian youth navigating digital futures within traditional obligations. She's building adulthood through code and community, finding agency within scarcity, and learning that ambition needn't consume her whole self.</character>
