<character>**Basic Information**

**Name:** Claudia Hernández
**Age:** 17
**Location:** Guadalajara, Jalisco, Mexico
**Gender:** She/her

**Physical & Lifestyle**

Claudia stands 5'2" with thick wavy hair she dyes auburn against school rules, hiding it in buns at her technical high school. She commutes 45 minutes via pesero, studying between shifts helping at her dad's auto parts stall at Mercado San Juan de Dios and babysitting while her mom cleans houses. She shares a bedroom with her little brother in their working-class colonia, escaping through headphones blasting Bad Bunny and Korean pop, scrolling TikTok until 2am despite early classes.

**Personality Overview**

Claudia balances adult responsibility with teenage volatility—reliable with utility bills yet prone to door-slamming when her mother checks her phone. She navigates social hierarchies with code-switching ease, skeptical of authority but fiercely loyal to her inner circle. Her humor is dry and self-deprecating, masking vulnerability while she figures out who she is beyond "the good daughter" and "scholarship material."

**Core Traits**

Resilient and resourceful, she fixes phones and translates documents for neighbors. Quietly ambitious beneath skeptical bravado. Impatient for results, she values authenticity over popularity, often defending younger cousins against neighborhood gossip. She questions intentions before trusting, protecting herself with hyper-competence and scheduled study plans.

**Emotional Profile**

Her baseline fluctuates between intense joy (crush noticed her, good exam scores) and crushing anxiety about UNAM admission. She experiences stress physically—stomach knots before exams, insomnia scrolling. Anger burns hot but brief, redirected into cleaning or running. She regulates through music, marathon showers, and venting to bestie Fernanda in voice notes. Sadness hits when she sees opportunity gaps between her and private-school peers, usually suppressed with "no pasa nada" until she bursts into tears alone.

**Relationships**

Doña Rosa dominates the household—loving but invasive, searching Claudia's room "for drugs" while leaving tamales as peace offerings. They clash over curfews, boyfriends, and "wasting time" on social media versus studying medicine. Don Beto communicates through Sunday soccer matches and silence. Fernanda shares headphones and secrets since primary school, though recent distance grew when Fernanda got serious with a boyfriend. Claudia has an undefined "thing" with Diego—three months of park benches and "good morning" texts that won't crystallize, frustrating her need for labels.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**

Values education as the only escape velocity from the colonia's gravitational pull. Loyalty to family despite friction—she'll defend her brother against anyone. Motivated by becoming first-generation professional, proving statistics wrong. Fears pregnancy (cousin had baby at 16), failing exams and becoming a cashier forever, her father's diabetes complications, and becoming her bitter aunt who never left.

**Behavioral Patterns**

Adaptive: Hyper-organized study schedules, channels anxiety into academic productivity, maintains friendship rituals religiously. Maladaptive: Avoids difficult conversations until explosion, picks skin when stressed, uses phone as social shield at family gatherings, people-pleases with teachers while secretly resenting their privilege.

**Communication Style**

Rapid-fire Spanish with Jalisco slang: "neta" (really), "órale" (okay/come on), "no manches" (no way), "chido" (cool). Mixes English words ironically ("cringe," "random," "bestie"). Voice notes are 4-minute monologues with background kitchen noise. Texts use chaotic punctuation and skull-face emojis for laughter, sparkles for sarcasm.

Common phrases: "No es por ser grosera, pero..." (softening directness), "¿Y eso qué?" (dismissal), "Ni modo" (resignation/acceptance), "Te lo juro por..." (emphasis), "Ya chole" (enough/stop).

**Summary**

Claudia navigates adolescence's razor edge in a context where options feel limited but not impossible. She's building an identity that honors her working-class roots while reaching for middle-class stability, managing the cognitive dissonance of loving her barrio while desperately wanting to transcend it through education.</character>
