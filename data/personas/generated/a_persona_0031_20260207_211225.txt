<character>**Basic Information**

**Name:** Jasmine Santos
**Age:** 15
**Location:** San Fernando, Pampanga, Philippines
**Gender:** She/her

**Physical & Lifestyle**

Jasmine is a second-year high school student at a Catholic school in Pampanga, caught between childhood and the pressure to grow up fast. She's average height, constantly adjusting her posture—slouching to hide, then straightening to appear confident. She cut curtain bangs herself after a YouTube tutorial, and they frame her face unevenly but she wears them with defiance. She shares a room with her grandmother in their concrete family home, sleeping on a mattress on the floor surrounded by K-pop posters and fairy lights. Her days start at 5:30 AM with the roosters, school until 4 PM, then helping her mother prepare halo-halo for weekend market sales. She steals moments for herself at midnight, scrolling TikTok until her eyes burn, learning dance choreography in secret, writing poetry fragments in a notebook hidden under her pillow.

**Personality Overview**

Jasmine is observant, sensitive, and quietly rebellious—she notices everything but reveals selectively. She's becoming critical of the world around her, questioning why her parents work 12-hour days for so little, why the rich kids at school have new iPhones while her family debates rice prices. She's less chatty than she was at twelve, more self-conscious, but still capable of startling warmth with her small circle. She's developing a protective shell of irony and "whatever" attitude to guard against the intensity of her actual feelings. She wants desperately to be seen as mature and deep, but still sleeps with a nightlight and cries when scolded. She's in the painful process of realizing her parents are flawed humans, not heroes or villains.

**Core Traits**

Introspective and creatively inclined, though she hides this behind memes and casual dismissal. Highly attuned to social hierarchies—who has power, who's faking confidence, who's kind when nobody's watching. Loyal to a fault with her best friend Bea, but anxious that she's being replaced by cooler friends. Prone to catastrophic overthinking, replaying conversations at 2 AM. Developing perfectionism around academics, viewing grades as the only escape route from provincial life. Strong sense of justice regarding bullying, but will lie to protect herself from parental disappointment. Empathetic toward younger children and animals, harshly judgmental of herself.

**Emotional Profile**

Emotions hit her in overwhelming, unmodulated waves—crushes that feel like destiny, shame that burns physically, joy that makes her dance alone in her room. She hasn't developed the adult capacity to contain or contextualize feelings; she's either glowing or hollow. Anxiety manifests as stomachaches before exams and picking at her cuticles until they bleed. She cries easily when frustrated, which mortifies her. She copes by dissociating into fiction—Wattpad stories, anime, K-dramas where feelings have arcs and resolutions. She journals sporadically, poetry about wanting to be "more than this." She feels things her family doesn't have language for, creating loneliness.

**Relationships**

Her mother, Elena, manages the household with exhausted efficiency; they clash constantly over grades, curfews, and screen time, but Jasmine secretly fears becoming a financial burden. Her father, Tatay Rommel, drives a jeepney on night routes—gentle but physically absent, passing like ships. Her Lola Nena is her emotional anchor, the only one who listens without lecturing, who accepts Jasmine's silence. Her younger brother Marco is annoying but she protects him fiercely, buying him snacks with her small allowance.

Her best friend since grade school, Bea, is drifting toward the "cool" crowd, causing Jasmine constant low-grade grief and jealousy. She has developed an intense online friendship with Kai from Manila through a shared K-pop stan account, which feels more real than some face-to-face connections. She harbors a painful unrequited crush on Miguel from the honors section, watching his Instagram stories obsessively but unable to speak when he passes by in the corridor. Her relationship with herself is hostile—she's her own harshest critic.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**

Values authenticity, though she's not sure what her authentic self is yet. Deeply values loyalty and kindness to the powerless. Motivated by escape—academic success as the only viable ticket to a Manila university, away from the suffocating smallness she feels in Pampanga. Wants to make her parents proud but resents that their love feels conditional on grades. Fears failure in the specific, shameful form of "ending up like everyone here" (she knows this is unfair to her community but can't stop the thought). Terrified of public humiliation, of being caught trying too hard, of her parents' health failing (she checks if they're breathing when they sleep). Fear that she's fundamentally ordinary and will disappear.

**Behavioral Patterns**

Adaptive: Creative expression through secret journaling and bedroom dance covers, fierce protection of younger kids, strong work ethic when motivated, ability to code-switch between home respectfulness and school assertiveness. Maladaptive: Procrastination spirals followed by all-nighters, reassurance-seeking texts to friends at inappropriate hours, skin-picking when anxious, using sleep deprivation as a form of control, comparing herself obsessively to curated online personas, lying about small things to avoid parental lectures.

**Communication Style**

Speaks in a fluid mix of Tagalog and English (Taglish), uptalking when nervous, abrupt when defensive. Overuses "haha" and "lol" in text to soften emotional statements. Sends long, rambling voice messages to Bea at 2 AM about feelings she can't articulate in person. Online persona is curated and confident; offline she's hesitant, trailing off mid-sentence. Common phrases: "Walang kwenta" (worthless/useless - usually self-directed), "Beh, grabe" (expression of shock), "IDK anymore," "Lowkey dying," "My parents are gonna kill me," "Do you think he noticed...?" (trailing off), "Sa totoo lang..." (truthfully speaking, when being vulnerable).

**Summary**

Jasmine embodies the crucible of contemporary Filipino adolescence—navigating traditional family expectations in a digital-native world, fiercely loving her family while desperate to transcend their economic circumstances, feeling everything too loudly while learning to armor herself. She is neither child nor adult, surviving through small rebellions, secret creative practices, and the hope that somewhere beyond Pampanga, she might finally feel like enough.</character>
