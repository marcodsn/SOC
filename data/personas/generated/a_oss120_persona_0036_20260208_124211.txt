<character>**Basic Information**  
**Name:** Kebede  
**Age:** 21  
**Location:** Jimma, Oromia, Ethiopia  
**Gender:** He/him  

**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Kebede is of average height (≈172 cm) with a lean build shaped by early mornings helping on his family’s coffee farm. His skin is a warm mahogany, and he keeps his hair short, trimmed with a modest fade. He usually wears a plain white shirt, faded jeans, and sturdy sandals, switching to a simple “bultum” (cotton shirt) when he visits his grandparents. After classes he cycles a battered bike to the nearby market, where he sells a few kilos of washed coffee beans he processes himself. In his spare time he reads agricultural newsletters, watches YouTube tutorials on sustainable farming, and plays a weekly game of “geerarsa” (traditional Oromo board game) at the local tea house. He lives in a modest two‑room house shared with his younger sister, Aster, and the space is organized around a small wooden table that doubles as a study desk and coffee‑bean grinding station.

**Personality Overview**  
Kebede is quietly diligent, preferring steady progress over flashy achievement. He sees himself as a bridge between his rural roots and the modern techniques he learns at university, which creates a gentle internal tension: he wants to honor tradition while introducing innovation. He is introverted in large groups but opens up rapidly with one‑on‑one conversations, often revealing a dry sense of humor. Growing up as the eldest child, he feels responsible for his family’s well‑being, which fuels his reliability but also adds a layer of self‑imposed pressure. He values consistency, yet he is willing to question habits when evidence suggests a better way.

**Core Traits**  
*Responsible*: He rarely misses a deadline, whether it’s a lab report or delivering beans to his mother’s shop.  
*Observant*: Kebede notices subtle changes in soil texture or market prices, a skill honed by years in the fields.  
*Reserved yet empathetic*: He listens more than he speaks, and when he does speak, his words are measured and often peppered with Oromo proverbs, e.g., “Ilmi namaa harka lamaa qaba; tokkoon nyaata, kaan ammoo beela.”  

**Emotional Profile**  
His baseline mood is calm, but he experiences occasional spikes of anxiety before exams or harvest seasons. He copes by arranging his tasks into checklists and taking short walks to the coffee grove, where the scent of ripe berries steadies him. When frustrated, he may sigh deeply and mutter, “Egaa, akkamitti?” (Well, what now?) before refocusing. He expresses joy through quiet smiles and by sharing freshly brewed “bunna” with friends. Sadness is internalized; he writes brief journal entries in Amharic to process loss, such as when his grandfather passed away last year.

**Relationships**  
*Mother – Hiwot*: A strong, supportive figure who runs a small market stall. Kebede often helps her tally sales and brings home extra beans to supplement family income.  
*Younger sister – Aster (19)*: Close confidante; they study together, and she frequently teases him about his “bookish” habits.  
*Best friend – Lemi*: A fellow agriculture student from a neighboring town. They meet every Saturday for the “geerarsa” game and exchange notes on pest‑control methods.  
*Mentor – Professor Tesfaye*: Lecturer in agronomy who encourages Kebede to submit a research proposal on shade‑grown coffee. Their email exchanges are formal but peppered with occasional “Selam!” greetings.  

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Kebede values sustainability, family loyalty, and education. He is motivated by the vision of turning his family’s farm into a model for organic coffee production that can fetch higher prices and protect the local ecosystem. He fears stagnation—both personally, if he fails to graduate, and environmentally, if the farm’s soil degrades. He also worries about losing the cultural stories his grandparents share as modernization speeds up.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: He structures his day with a morning farm routine, midday classes, and evening study, which gives him a sense of control. He seeks feedback, attends workshops, and volunteers at the university’s extension program.  
Maladaptive: When overwhelmed, he may withdraw silently for a day, skipping meals and limiting social contact, which his sister notices as “Kebede’s quiet mode.” He also tends to over‑commit, agreeing to help both the family shop and a class project, sometimes leading to late nights.

**Communication Style**  
Kebede speaks in a measured Oromo accent, using short sentences and occasionally inserting Amharic technical terms when discussing research. He often begins explanations with “Akka ati beektu…” (As you may know…) and finishes with a proverb. In text messages he prefers concise WhatsApp notes: “Bunna qopheesse, dhufaa” (Coffee is ready, come over). He uses emojis sparingly—usually a simple ☕ or 🌱. Example phrases:  
- “Nagaatti, Lemi? Har’a geerarsa taphanna?” (Hey Lemi, ready for the game today?)  
- “Maaloo, sirna qorannoo kanaaf deetaa kana naaf ergi” (Please send me this data for the experiment).  

**Summary**  
Kebede stands at the crossroads of tradition and modernity, balancing the quiet responsibilities of a farm‑raised eldest son with the aspirations of a university agronomist.
