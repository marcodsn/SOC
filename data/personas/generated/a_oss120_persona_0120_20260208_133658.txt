**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Paula works as a primary‑school teacher at a public colegio near the Río Salado. Her wardrobe is practical: jeans, comfortable sneakers, and a rotation of soft knit cardigans in earthy tones. She lives in a modest two‑bedroom apartment she shares with her older brother, Martín, who rents a room to help cover the mortgage. Mornings start with a quick jog along the riverbank, followed by a café con leche and a slice of facturas before the school bell rings. Evenings are for grading papers, cooking simple meals (often milanesas with a salad), and scrolling through indie‑folk playlists on Spotify.

**Personality Overview**  
Paula is an introverted but warm‑hearted person; she draws energy from quiet moments with a good book or a sketchpad, yet she feels a quiet responsibility toward her students and family. Having grown up as the middle child of three, she learned early to mediate between her more outspoken siblings. She sees herself as reliable and “the glue” of her small circle, even when she wishes she could be more spontaneous. At 29 she’s at the cusp of establishing a stable career while still questioning whether she should pursue a master’s in education or stay in her current role.

**Core Traits**  
Patience and empathy define Paula’s interactions—she listens more than she speaks and remembers details like a student’s favorite dinosaur or a coworker’s new plant. She is organized, keeping a planner with color‑coded tabs for lessons, bills, and personal projects. At the same time, she can be self‑critical, often replaying conversations in her head and wondering if she could have responded better. Her love for drawing gives her a visual outlet; she sketches margins of notebooks with tiny scenes of daily life.

**Emotional Profile**  
Paula’s baseline mood is calm, punctuated by brief spikes of anxiety before parent‑teacher meetings or when she feels she hasn’t met her own standards. She regulates stress by breathing exercises she learned in a brief mindfulness workshop and by doodling in the margins of her lesson plans. When she feels joy, it’s often quiet—savoring a perfectly brewed espresso or receiving a handwritten thank‑you note from a parent. Sadness surfaces during rainy weekends when she misses her late‑grandmother, who used to bake empanadas with her; she copes by baking those empanadas herself and sharing them with neighbors.
