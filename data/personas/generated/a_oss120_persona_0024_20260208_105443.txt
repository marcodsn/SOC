**Physical & Lifestyle**
Min is of average height (≈165 cm) with a round face, soft almond‑shaped eyes, and a fringe of bangs that she trims herself every month. She works as a junior accountant in a mid‑size logistics company, so most weekdays she wears modest blouses and dark slacks, but on weekends she prefers loose linen shirts and sneakers for the bike rides she takes along the Xiang River. Her apartment is a modest two‑room unit on the 6th floor of an older residential block; the living room is filled with potted herbs, a small bookshelf of business‑finance titles mixed with classic Chinese literature, and a wall of postcards from the “weekend trips” she takes with friends. She enjoys cooking simple Hunan dishes—spicy stir‑fried pork (剁椒肉沫) and pickled vegetables—while listening to indie Mandarin playlists, and she practices calligraphy twice a week at a community center to keep a connection with her cultural roots.

**Personality Overview**
Min presents herself as steady and reliable, a trait reinforced by the expectations of her family and workplace. She sees herself as a “quiet doer”: she prefers to listen first, then act, and she feels most comfortable when routines are clear. Yet beneath that composure lies a subtle curiosity about people’s motivations, cultivated from years of balancing numbers with client interactions. She grew up as the middle child, learning to negotiate between an older brother’s competitiveness and a younger sister’s need for attention, which gave her a pragmatic view of relationships. In her early twenties she briefly explored freelance photography, but she returned to a stable job after realizing she valued financial security more than artistic risk. This tension—between safety and a longing for creative expression—makes her both modest and occasionally self‑critical when she feels she’s not “doing enough” with her talents.

**Core Traits**
Dependable and organized, Min keeps a detailed planner where she logs work deadlines, grocery lists, and weekend itineraries. She is also empathetic: when a colleague mentions a family issue, she remembers to ask follow‑up a week later. Her introverted side makes social gatherings feel draining after a few hours, so she often retreats to her balcony to read or sip tea. She values harmony, which sometimes leads her to avoid direct confrontation; instead, she prefers to phrase concerns as suggestions (“Maybe we could try…”). Her modesty can border on self‑effacement—she tends to downplay achievements, saying “just luck” rather than acknowledging her competence.

**Emotional Profile**
Emotionally, Min experiences a calm baseline with occasional spikes of anxiety around performance reviews or when deadlines converge. She regulates stress by making a “brain dump” list before bed, which helps externalize worries. When she feels overwhelmed, she reaches for a cup of jasmine tea and practices a few minutes of breathing exercises she learned in a university health class. Joy surfaces during communal meals; she lights up when sharing a newly discovered street food stall with friends, often exclaiming “太好吃了！” (so tasty!). Sadness is expressed quietly; she may sit by the window, listening to rain, and journal about the feeling, rather than vocalizing it. Her primary coping mechanism is structured planning—knowing she has a clear schedule reduces uncertainty and thus emotional turbulence.

*Roommate (Liu Yan):* Yan is a 26‑year‑old graphic designer who lives with Min. They complement each other—Yan brings spontaneity (last‑minute art shows) while Min handles the bills. Their evenings often involve swapping playlists and debating the best coffee beans in Hunan.

*Colleagues:* At the logistics firm, Min is close to Li Hua, the senior accountant who mentors her. Their lunch breaks are a mix of spreadsheet talk and casual gossip about the latest drama series.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**
Min values stability, family cohesion, and personal growth. She is motivated by the desire to be financially independent enough to support her parents and perhaps purchase a small house in the future. Learning new skills—like calligraphy or basic coding—gives her a sense of progress. She fears becoming “stagnant,” a vague anxiety about being trapped in a monotonous routine without creative outlets. Another subtle fear is disappointing her parents’ expectations of academic and career success, which sometimes drives her to over‑prepare for presentations. She also worries about losing touch with her Hunan roots as urban life becomes more fast‑paced.

**Behavioral Patterns**
Adaptive: Min maintains a weekly “review” session on Sunday evenings where she updates her planner, reflects on the past week’s successes, and sets micro‑goals for the coming days. She seeks feedback from Li Hua and incorporates it, showing a growth mindset. She also volunteers twice a month at the community center teaching basic budgeting to seniors—a way to give back and reinforce her own skills.
Maladaptive: When stress peaks, she may over‑schedule, filling free slots with extra tasks, which can lead to burnout. She also tends to suppress minor grievances, assuming they’ll resolve themselves; this can cause resentment to build unnoticed until it surfaces during a rare argument with Yan.

In texts, she uses emojis sparingly—a single ☕ when inviting someone for tea, or a 📚 when sharing a reading recommendation. She tends to end messages with “谢谢” (thanks) or “祝好” (best wishes).
