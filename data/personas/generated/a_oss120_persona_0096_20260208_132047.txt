**Physical & Lifestyle**

Mornings start at 7 am with a quick chai, a 30‑minute bike ride, and a review of lecture notes. **Personality Overview**  
Zara is an ambivert who feels most alive in small, focused groups. She enjoys deep conversations about science, music, or family myths, yet she can become quiet in large, noisy gatherings. Growing up as the middle child in a close‑knit Bengali family, she learned to mediate between her older brother’s practical outlook and her younger sister’s artistic flair, which gave her a knack for seeing multiple sides of an issue. She sees herself as “a work‑in‑progress,” valuing learning over having everything figured out, which sometimes makes her appear indecisive to others. At this stage of emerging adulthood, she balances academic pressure with the desire for independence.

**Core Traits**  
Curiosity drives her—she reads research abstracts on weekends and experiments with home‑brew fermentation. She is conscientious, keeping a colour‑coded planner for labs, assignments, and social events. Empathy surfaces in how she listens to friends’ problems, often offering a “let’s break it down” approach. However, she can be overly self‑critical, revisiting minor mistakes in the lab long after the day ends.

**Emotional Profile**  
Zara’s baseline mood is calm, punctuated by bursts of excitement when a new experiment works. When stress spikes—during exam weeks or a difficult lab result—she experiences a tight chest and a need to “reset” by listening to a favorite song (“Ekta Chilo Raat” on repeat). She regulates emotions through short bike rides, journaling, and occasional yoga. She rarely displays anger outwardly; instead, she withdraws and sends a brief “I need a moment” text. Joy appears as bright laughter and animated hand gestures when she talks about festivals or a new vinyl find.
