**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Myra is 162 cm tall, with a slim build and a habit of tucking her dark, wavy hair behind her ears when she concentrates. Her mornings begin with a cup of brewed Assam tea, a short walk to the nearby Hooghly River, and a quick scan of the *Anandabazar Patrika* before catching the local bus to the school where she teaches Bengali literature. **Personality Overview**  
Myra is an introverted but warm‑hearted “quiet hub” of her friend group: she listens more than she speaks, yet when she does share, it’s often laced with dry humor. She sees herself as a “steady hand” that others can rely on, though she sometimes doubts whether she’s “enough” to inspire her students. Growing up as the eldest child in a middle‑class Bengali family, she learned to balance personal aspirations with family expectations, a tension that fuels both her perseverance and occasional self‑criticism. She is in the early adult developmental stage where career identity and relational intimacy intersect.

**Core Traits**  
Reliability is Myra’s hallmark; colleagues know she will cover a class at the drop of a hat. She also possesses a strong sense of aesthetic curiosity, evident in her love for vintage postcards and classic film posters. While organized in lesson planning, she can be chaotic about keeping her personal space tidy, often leaving books stacked haphazardly on the bedside table. Her humor is understated, usually delivered as a single‑line observation: “If only exams could be as short as a bhapa pitha.”

**Emotional Profile**  
Emotionally, Myra experiences a low‑grade anxiety that peaks before parent‑teacher meetings or when she must speak publicly. She manages this by rehearsing in front of the bathroom mirror and using breath‑counting apps. Joy surfaces during family meals; she lights up when her mother praises her cooking, often saying, “Your khichuri is my favorite comfort.” Sadness tends to be internalized; she may stare at rain‑drenched windows for a few minutes before writing in her journal. She rarely displays anger outwardly, preferring to write a concise note to the person involved.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Myra values education, cultural heritage, and familial loyalty. She is motivated by the desire to make literature accessible to her students, hoping to spark the same curiosity she felt reading *Pather Panchali* as a child. She fears becoming “invisible” in the crowd—either at work, where senior teachers dominate, or in her family, where younger relatives’ achievements sometimes eclipse hers. A lingering fear is that she might never travel beyond West Bengal, limiting her exposure to broader literary traditions.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptively, she keeps a daily planner, uses color‑coded tabs for lesson plans, and practices mindfulness meditation for ten minutes each night. Maladaptively, she tends to over‑commit to extracurricular clubs, leading to occasional sleeplessness, and she postpones difficult conversations by sending polite but vague texts like “Let’s catch up soon.”

**Communication Style**  
Myra’s spoken language blends Bengali and English, often slipping into “Benglish” mid‑sentence. She speaks at a measured pace, punctuating points with gentle hand gestures. In texting she prefers full sentences, correct punctuation, and occasional emojis: “Got the lesson plan ready 😊. See you at 4 pm?” Common phrases include, “Ami ektu bhabte pari?” (“Can I think about it a bit?”) and, “Bujhte parchi, but…” (“I understand, but…”).
