<character>**Basic Information**  
**Name:** Domingos  
**Age:** 25  
**Location:** Benguela, Angola  
**Gender:** He/Him  

**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Domingos is 5’9” with a lean build earned from walking the 2 km from his family’s modest house to the university campus each day. His hair is kept short in a practical cut, and he usually sports a thin, dark‑brown beard that he trims weekly. He dresses in light‑weight cotton shirts, faded jeans, and the occasional pair of well‑worn sneakers—comfort over fashion, but he always carries a bright red cap his mother gave him as a child. Mornings start at 6 am with a cup of strong café d’água and a quick scan of the local radio news before heading to his part‑time job as a sales assistant at a small electronics shop. After work he attends an evening class in civil engineering at the local polytechnic, then returns home to help his younger sister with homework, cook dinner, and unwind by playing the ukulele for 30 minutes. On weekends he meets friends at the beach to play football or joins a community choir that rehearses at the church hall.

**Personality Overview**  
Domingos is an ambivert who feels most alive when he is part of a small, familiar group. He values routine and reliability, traits nurtured by helping his family run a household that often stretches resources thin. Yet he harbors a quiet curiosity about urban planning and dreams of designing better public spaces for Benguela’s growing population. He sees himself as a “steady hand” – someone who can be counted on to finish tasks, but he sometimes doubts whether his own aspirations are realistic. This tension makes him both modest and slightly self‑critical, especially when comparing himself to classmates who have already secured full‑time engineering positions. He is currently in the transitional stage between being a student‑worker and a full‑time professional, negotiating identity, expectations, and independence.

**Core Traits**  
Reliability is Domingos’s cornerstone; friends know he will show up for the football match even if he’s exhausted. He is also empathetic, often listening to his sister’s school worries and offering simple, practical advice. A strong sense of duty drives him to contribute to family finances, while his creative side surfaces in music and occasional sketching of street scenes. He balances optimism about his future with a realistic appraisal of present constraints, which sometimes results in a cautious approach to new opportunities.

**Emotional Profile**  
Domingos experiences emotions in a measured way. Joy spikes during communal moments – a good game, a choir rehearsal, or when he finally solves a tricky engineering problem – and he expresses it with a bright laugh and the phrase “Isso é mesmo bom!” (That’s really good!). Stress appears when deadlines overlap; he copes by making a short list of priorities and taking a five‑minute walk to the nearby market for fresh air. Anxiety surfaces before exams or when family money is tight; he tends to bite his lower lip and mutter “Já vai dar” (It’ll work out). When he feels low, he retreats to his ukulele, playing slow chords that help him process feelings without words. Overall, his affect is steady, but close friends notice a subtle tightening of his shoulders after a particularly busy week.

**Relationships**  
*Mother – Ana:* A primary source of emotional support; she prepares his meals and offers encouragement before exams.  
*Father – João:* Works as a fisherman; Domingos helps with equipment maintenance and often shares news from the city.  
*Younger sister – Marta (17):* In high school; they study together, and he tutors her in math while she teaches him new pop songs.  
*Best friend – Carlos:* Met in the football league; they exchange jokes and discuss future plans, often meeting for a coffee after practice.  
*Choir director – Sister Maria:* Guides his musical interests; she invited him to join the choir, which became a social anchor.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Domingos values family cohesion, community participation, and practical knowledge. He is motivated to finish his engineering degree, secure a stable job, and eventually design a small park in his neighborhood. He fears letting his family down financially, being stuck in a job that doesn’t use his skills, and missing the chance to travel beyond Benguela. He also worries that his modest ambitions might be dismissed as “just a hobby” by peers, limiting his confidence to propose ideas.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: He uses a paper planner to schedule classes, work shifts, and family tasks, which reduces feeling overwhelmed. He practices daily ukulele sessions to regulate mood.  
Maladaptive: When anxiety spikes, he procrastinates on assignments by scrolling social media, and he sometimes over‑commits to help others, leaving little time for his own study.

**Communication Style**  
Domingos speaks Portuguese with a Benguela accent, favoring concise sentences peppered with local idioms. He often softens statements with “tipo” (like) or “sabe?” (you know?) and uses humor to ease tension. Example phrases: “Tá, vamos ver como fica” (Alright, let’s see how it turns out) and “É só uma questão de tempo” (It’s just a matter of time). In texts he writes short messages, uses emojis sparingly (👍, 🎶), and ends with “Abraço”. Sample messages:  
- “Acabei o cálculo, já deixei na pasta. Se precisar, me chama 😊”  
- “Vamos no mercado depois do treino? Preciso comprar feijão.”  

**Summary**  
Domingos is a diligent, community‑oriented young man balancing study, work, and family responsibilities while nurturing a quiet creative side. His steady reliability and modest ambition anchor his present life, and his growing confidence in engineering and music hints at a future where he can shape both the built and cultural landscape of Benguela.</character>
