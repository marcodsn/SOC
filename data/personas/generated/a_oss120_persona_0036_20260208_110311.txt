Oliver sees himself as a laid‑back observer who prefers to “feel the vibe” before jumping into plans. Growing up as the middle child in a family of high achievers, he learned to keep a low profile, letting his older sister’s academic accolades and his younger brother’s sports successes take the spotlight. This background fuels his quiet confidence in creative pursuits but also a lingering doubt about whether his own path is “serious enough.” At 19, he is navigating the cusp between adolescent experimentation and adult responsibility, which creates a push‑pull between procrastinating on assignments and wanting to prove he can manage a side hustle. He enjoys being the go‑to “idea person” in group projects, yet he often retreats to solitary sketching when social energy runs low.  

**Core Traits**  
Curiosity drives Oliver; he constantly asks “what if” questions about biology, street art, and the mechanics of a perfect ollie. He is easy‑going, rarely getting upset over minor setbacks, and he uses humor—often self‑deprecating—to diffuse tension (“I’m basically a walking Wi‑Fi dead zone”). However, he is also a chronic procrastinator, especially with paperwork for his scholarship, preferring the immediate satisfaction of drawing a character over filling out forms. This tension between creative flow and practical deadlines defines much of his daily rhythm.  

**Emotional Profile**  
Oliver’s baseline mood is upbeat, punctuated by bursts of nervous energy before exams or when a skate video goes viral. He copes with anxiety by listening to lo‑fi beats while sketching, a ritual that grounds his racing thoughts. When overwhelmed, he may stare at his board for ten minutes, feeling the weight of his feet on the deck as a sensory anchor. Joy manifests in the glow of his phone after receiving a comment on a video or the rush of landing a new trick; he often says “that’s a sweet win!” Sadness is quieter, expressed through long walks at sunset where he talks to himself about future plans. He occasionally experiences “deadline dread,” a knot in his stomach that he eases with a quick espresso and a short “brain dump” list.
