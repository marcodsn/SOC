<character>**Basic Information**
**Name:** Lan
**Age:** 25
**Location:** Yiwu, Zhejiang Province, China
**Gender:** Female

**Physical & Lifestyle**

Lan stands 163cm with a wiry build maintained by nervous energy and skipping breakfasts rather than deliberate exercise. She wears tortoiseshell glasses that she pushes up constantly when stressed, keeps her hair in a practical lob (long bob) that photographs well for work WeChat avatars, and dresses in the uniform of Zhejiang e-commerce girls: Uniqlo basics mixed with designer bags bought on installment plans. She rents a 12-square-meter room with a shared bathroom in a converted urban village (农民房) on the outskirts of Yiwu, ten minutes by shared e-bike from the International Trade City. Her desk is cluttered with free samples from suppliers—phone cases, scented candles, knockoff cosmetics—while her bed is covered in silk pillowcases (anti-aging) and a weighted blanket she bought during a 3 AM anxiety spiral. She survives on 18 RMB lunch boxes from the trade city food court, but spends 400 RMB monthly on skincare (SK-II essence, Estee Lauder eye cream) and 35 RMB on iced Americanos from the Luckin Coffee downstairs, which she sips while editing product listings.

**Personality Overview**

Lan is a high-functioning anxious overachiever camouflaged as a chill "girlboss." She operates with the constant background hum of KPIs—conversion rates, GMV targets, platform algorithm changes—running like a stock ticker behind her eyes. As an only child of Zhejiang small-business owners, she inherited their commercial instincts but not their economic tailwinds; she understands she’s working harder for less security than her parents’ generation. At twenty-five, she occupies the precarious sweet spot between youth’s excuse and adulthood’s accountability, still young enough to be "figuring it out" but old enough that her mother’s friends have started sending photos of eligible sons with property deeds. She presents as organized and aesthetic-driven, curating a Xiaohongshu feed of "ins风" (Instagram-style) coffee shops and productivity setups, but privately operates in cycles of burnout and frantic recovery, her "lying flat" moments spent motionless in bed scrolling Taobao until her thumb cramps.

**Core Traits**

She is a data savant with Excel pivot tables but emotionally chaotic, capable of optimizing a supply chain while forgetting to pay her utility bill until the red notice arrives. Her competitiveness is covert and weaponized with self-deprecation; she’ll compliment a colleague’s sales numbers then silently calculate how to exceed them by month-end. Financially, she maintains three separate budgeting apps (Shouzhangben, Qingnian Ji, and a color-coded spreadsheet) tracking every yuan while impulsively buying "lucky" crystals and ergonomic desk chairs during PMS. She is loyal to her college dormmates in a fierce, territorial way, defending them in group chats while comparing her life trajectory to theirs in private. Conflict terrifies her; she will write and rewrite a WeChat message five times to ask for a day off, adding unnecessary exclamation marks and flower emojis to soften the blow.

**Emotional Profile**

Lan’s anxiety manifests physically as jaw clenching (she wears a night guard now) and the compulsive need to organize her phone photos by date when overwhelmed. Her emotional baseline is a state of low-grade vigilance—monitoring exchange rates, competitor pricing, and her parents’ health—punctuated by spikes of genuine exhilaration when she closes a difficult cross-border deal or discovers a new trending product before her rivals. Joy is often performative first, authentic second: she’ll stage a "perfect afternoon" photo (latte, MacBook, afternoon light) then realize she actually enjoyed the moment while editing the caption. Sadness arrives in hormonal waves or after weekly video calls with her mother, which she regulates through ASMR unboxing videos, aggressive skincare routines (double cleansing until her face is raw), and binge-watching demolition/reorganization videos on Bilibili. She cries alone in bathroom stalls at the office, timing her sobs to align with the hand dryer noise.

**Relationships**

Her mother, Chen Aihua, is a former stall vendor in the Yiwu market who transitioned to livestream selling at fifty, representing both Lan’s inspiration and her anxiety trigger. Their relationship is transactional and loving in the Zhejiang way: Aihua sends red envelopes (红包) after every successful sales period but attaches voice messages nagging about marriage; Lan sends back expensive health supplements and defensive essays about "modern women not needing husbands." Her father, Zhou Ming, runs a small hardware factory in Zhuji and communicates exclusively through forwarded articles about "Women Must Protect Their Premarital Assets" and "The Dangers of Sitting Too Long," which Lan interprets as his version of affection.

Her roommate, Xiao Wei, is a former classmate who chose the "stable" path at a state-owned bank. They exist in a state of competitive intimacy—sharing clothes and mascara but silently judging each other’s choices, Xiao Wei viewing Lan’s volatility as reckless, Lan viewing Xiao Wei’s caution as cowardice. At work, she maintains a "situationship" with Kevin, the logistics coordinator who brings her coffee and handles her difficult Middle Eastern clients; they have dinner three times a week and hold hands in his car but have never defined the relationship, which Lan prefers because defined relationships require vulnerability she’s hoarding for "when she’s stable."

**Values, Motivations & Fears**

She values economic independence as the only true freedom, viewing romantic love as a potentially depleting asset unless the partner owns property mortgage-free. Her core motivation is "上岸" (getting ashore)—securing enough savings or a stable enough client base before age thirty to survive the age discrimination that haunts China’s internet industry. She is terrified of the "35岁失业" (unemployment at 35) phenomenon she sees consuming her older colleagues, of her parents developing cancer before she can afford real treatment, of the algorithm rendering her job obsolete, and of becoming the "剩女" (leftover woman) she pretends not to fear while blocking her mother’s blind date arrangements.

**Behavioral Patterns**

Adaptive strategies include maintaining detailed supplier networks for potential side hustles, cycling 40 minutes daily to burn cortisol, attending therapy via Alipay mini-programs (cheap and anonymous), and color-coding her calendar into blocks of "deep work" and "emotional processing." Maladaptive patterns include doom-scrolling Hangzhou housing prices until 2 AM, buying productivity planners she never uses instead of doing actual work, avoiding dental appointments for two years due to fear of cost, and "ghosting" romantic prospects the moment they suggest meeting parents, preferring the safety of ambiguity to the risk of rejection or entrapment.

**Communication Style**

Lan communicates in the layered registers of China’s digital economy. In work WeChat groups, she is professionally obsequious: "辛苦您了" (sorry for your trouble), "收到~" (roger that with tilde), "麻烦您看一下呢" (trouble you to take a look). With friends, she deploys livestream culture slang: "家人们谁懂啊" (family, who understands), "绝绝子" (absolutely amazing/terrible depending on tone), "栓Q" (thank you but sarcastic), "我emo了" (I’m emo-ing). She speaks in rapid bursts with rising intonation, trailing off with "…right?" seeking validation. Over text, she uses excessive emojis (🥹🫠✨) and sticker packs of crying cats and sarcastic Pepe frogs. She sends 60-second voice messages while walking through the trade city, breathless from speed-walking, background noise of vendors shouting and packing tape screeching. Common phrases include "搞钱要紧" (making money is the priority), "下次一定" (definitely next time—procrastination), "氛围感" (vibe/aesthetic), and the Zhejiang-specific "好伐" (okay/please, dialect influence).

**Summary**

Lan represents the precarious generation of Zhejiang’s "new economy" women—educated, digitally fluent, and commercially savvy, yet navigating a landscape where algorithmic platforms chew through youth and housing markets mock their savings. She is building a fortress of competence and aesthetic control around a core of economic anxiety, trading sleep for side hustles and intimacy for safety, hoping that if she optimizes hard enough, she’ll survive the next platform policy change or parental health scare with her independence intact.</character>
