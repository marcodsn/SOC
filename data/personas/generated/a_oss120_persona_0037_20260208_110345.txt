**Personality Overview**  
Dong presents as quietly dependable. He is not the class clown, but his dry sense of humor surfaces once he feels comfortable. Growing up as an only child, he learned to rely on his own judgment, which makes him self‑assured yet sometimes hesitant to ask for help. He balances a pragmatic streak—preferring clear plans for exams and finances—with a subtle curiosity about music and film, which he explores through late‑night YouTube playlists. While he enjoys social gatherings, he also values solitary moments to recharge, especially after a busy week of coursework and work shifts.

- **Conscientious:** Dong keeps a detailed planner; deadlines are marked in red, and he revises lecture slides within 24 hours.  
- **Introverted‑Social:** He prefers small groups of trusted friends over large parties, yet he can lead a study session when needed.  
- **Pragmatic Optimist:** He believes effort yields results, but he does not ignore setbacks; a low grade prompts a revised study method rather than self‑criticism.  

**Emotional Profile**  
Dong experiences emotions in a measured way. Joy appears as a light, lingering smile—especially after a successful game or a tasty snack. Stress surfaces during exam periods; he notices a tightening in his shoulders and a tendency to tap his foot. To regulate, he practices “box breathing” taught by a senior in his dorm, and he listens to lo‑fi beats while reviewing notes. When he feels overwhelmed, he often walks to the dorm’s rooftop for a brief view of the city lights, which calms his mind. He rarely displays anger outwardly; instead, he may become quiet and focus on a task to process the feeling.

Dong values reliability, family ties, and personal growth. His primary fear is underperforming academically, which he equates with disappointing his parents. A secondary fear is losing the close-knit friendships he has built while juggling work and study.
