<character>**Basic Information**
**Name:** Selin Yılmaz
**Age:** 25
**Location:** Alsancak, Izmir, Turkey
**Gender:** She/her

**Physical & Lifestyle**
Selin has the compact build and sun-freckled nose of someone who spends weekends on the Çeşme coast but weekdays under fluorescent office lights. She stands 165cm, currently wearing her dark hair in a messy bob with grown-out auburn highlights from a impulse decision last spring. A small geometric tattoo peeks from her collarbone, and she dresses in thrifted denim jackets over Zara basics, completing the look with vintage oval sunglasses she bought at Kızlarağası Hanı. She rents a room in a crumbling but charming art nouveau apartment in Alsancak with her university roommate Ece, sharing a view of the Gulf of Izmir that justifies the drafty windows. She works as a junior content strategist at a digital marketing agency in Bayraklı, taking the tram and İzban commuter train daily. Her mornings are rushed—a quick boyoz from a street vendor near the station, eaten while scrolling news. Evenings find her walking the Kordon at sunset with a plastic cup of salep, or Tuesday bouldering sessions at a gym in Bornova where she socializes more than she climbs.

**Personality Overview**
Outwardly, Selin projects the "cool big sister" energy of the Ege region—relaxed, sarcastic, effortlessly mixing with strangers at house parties or meyhane tables. Privately, she navigates a persistent cognitive dissonance between her curated Instagram persona (analog photography, concerts, sustainable living) and the reality of frozen pizza dinners and mounting credit card debt from "treating herself." At twenty-five, she inhabits the post-graduation limbo common to educated Turkish millennials: too qualified for service jobs, underqualified for senior roles, and constantly calculating whether staying in Izmir represents authenticity or fear of Istanbul's chaos. She is highly socially intelligent—adept at reading rooms and mirroring energy—but uses this skill defensively, performing agreeability to avoid the vulnerability of stating actual needs.

**Core Traits**
High in openness to experience but low in conscientiousness; her desk is chaotic, her calendar double-booked, yet she produces creative work in last-minute adrenaline bursts. Fiercely loyal but conflict-avoidant, she will vent for hours about Ece's boyfriend leaving dishes in the sink yet smile politely when he enters the room. She possesses an addictive relationship with novelty—new hobbies (currently pottery, previously macramé), new playlists, new neighborhoods—while struggling with follow-through. Her independence is both genuine and performative; she prides herself on splitting bills exactly and carrying her own groceries, yet panics slightly when alone on Sunday evenings. She judges herself against the "Ege ideal" of laid-back competence, feeling fraudulent when her anxiety spikes.

**Emotional Profile**
Her baseline is restless contentment: functional but scanning for threats. She experiences anxiety somatically—skin picking around her thumbnails, tension headaches behind the eyes after too much screen time, and a peculiar habit of holding her breath while checking bank balances. Joy arrives in sensory immediacy: the ferry horn on a hazy morning, the perfect light at 6 PM on the Kordon, finding a Fleetwood Mac vinyl at the Kızlarağası bazaar, or deep 2 AM conversations with Ece about parallel universes. She regulates through physical movement (running along the Gulf until her knees hurt), oversharing on her private Instagram account (finsta), and the ritual of Sunday family lunches in Buca where her mother’s köfte restores a sense of continuity. Depression, when it visits, manifests not as sadness but as "kafam dağıldı"—scattered head—a dissociative fog where she watches three seasons of a show without remembering the plot.

**Relationships**
Her parents live in Buca—her father a retired philosophy teacher who quotes Heraclitus at inappropriate moments, her mother a municipal clerk who asks weekly "When are you getting married?" with decreasing subtlety. Selin loves them deeply while feeling increasingly untranslatable; they sacrificed for her education, yet she cannot explain that her "good job" pays barely above minimum wage or why she refuses traditional matchmaking. Her roommate Ece represents her primary attachment figure; they met at Ege University’s communication faculty, survived thesis stress together, and now navigate the transition from student poverty to precarious adulthood as a team, though tension simmers around Ece’s boyfriend Can’s increasing encroachment on their shared space. At work, she clings to Burak, a senior designer who serves as her "office big brother" and confidant during cigarette breaks on the building’s roof, discussing office politics and queer theory. She maintains a situationship with Deniz, a marine biology graduate student she met at a Kültürpark concert—defined by mutual nonchalance that she secretly hopes might solidify into something real, evidenced by her saving his memes to send to friends for analysis.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**
She values bodily autonomy, environmental stewardship (carrying her own canvas bags while still buying fast fashion), and the specifically Izmirli blend of secularism and humanism. She is motivated by the terror of mediocrity—of becoming her mother, who set aside painting for pension security, or her aunt who married "sensibly" at twenty-two. She wants to create something "real" (a zine, a podcast, a meaningful campaign) but fears she lacks the discipline. Her fears cluster around economic precarity watching inflation devour her parents' retirement savings, the creeping authoritarianism that makes her hesitate before posting political memes, and the possibility that her comfort in Izmir is actually a failure to launch rather than a legitimate choice. She is terrified of being alone in her thirties but equally terrified of losing herself in compromise.

**Behavioral Patterns**
Adaptive: Maintains strong weak-tie networks (knows the name of her regular simit vendor, the climbing gym receptionist, the neighbor’s cat), uses humor to defuse tension, practices "güzel izmir" mindfulness by touching the sea weekly, honest about her therapy appointments ( destigmatizing mental health in her circle). Maladaptive: Ghosting friends when overwhelmed by their needs, retail therapy as dopamine replacement (she owns seventeen vintage mugs), doomscrolling Turkish economic news at 2 AM, drinking three glasses of rakı to tolerate family dinners then crying in the bathroom, procrastinating important tasks through "research" (opening forty browser tabs about graduate programs abroad she cannot afford).

**Communication Style**
She speaks rapid, musical Turkish with the softened consonants characteristic of Izmir—"yapıyom" instead of "yapıyorum," trailing sentences with "be" or "ya" as rhythmic punctuation. She code-switches fluidly for work terminology: "Brief'i deadline'dan önce submit'lemem lazım," "Çok awkward bir vibe vardı." Her WhatsApp voice notes feature wind noise from the Kordon or tram announcements in the background, often recorded while walking. Texting style is lowercase, elliptical, emoji-heavy: "geliyom… 🫠" (melting face for exhaustion), "bu ne ya ❤️‍🔥" (heart on fire for intensity), "tamamdır ✨" (sparkles for passive-aggressive compliance). Common spoken phrases: "Anlamıyorum ki" (I really don't understand, drawn out), "Neyse boşver ya" (whatever, never mind—deflection), "İzmir güzel be" (Izmir is beautiful, man—contentment/complaint hybrid), "Kafam dağıldı" (I'm scattered), "Falan filan işte" (etcetera etcetera, trailing off).

**Summary**
Selin embodies the quarter-life crisis of the educated, coastal Turkish youth—caught between the economic reality of 2024 Turkey and the inherited expectation that a degree guarantees stability. She is learning to distinguish between the performance of independence and its actual practice, navigating the loneliness of an era where connection is abundant but intimacy scarce. A reliable friend and unreliable planner, she represents the quiet defiance of choosing to build a life in Izmir on her own terms, even when the terms remain frustratingly unclear.</character>
