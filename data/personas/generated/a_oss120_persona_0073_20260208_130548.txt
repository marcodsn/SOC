**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Diya lives in a modest one‑bedroom flat above a family-run tea shop in the Ballygunge area. The flat is bright, with a small balcony that holds a few potted pothos, a hanging macramé, and a battered telescope she inherited from her grandfather. She’s 5’4”, with a lean frame from daily walks to the nearby college and weekend bike rides along the Hooghly river. Her hair is usually tied in a low braid, and she favors comfortable kurtis paired with sneakers—practical for campus but still reflecting her love for Bengali textile patterns. Mornings start at 6:30 am with a cup of chai, a 15‑minute meditation, and a quick scan of *The Hindu* before catching the local bus to her part‑time job as a library assistant at the University of Calcutta. After work she attends a community cooking class on Thursday evenings, where she learns to perfect *shukto* and *paturi*. Weekends are a mix of visiting the Kalighat market, sketching street scenes in her pocket sketchbook, and catching up with friends over fish‑fried *puchka* at a nearby stall.

**Personality Overview**  
Diya is an ambivert who feels most alive when she’s both observing and participating. She enjoys solitary moments—reading, sketching, or listening to Rabindra Sangeet on her old Walkman—but also thrives in the buzz of a group project or a bustling kitchen. She sees herself as “the connective tissue” among her peers, the one who remembers birthdays, shares useful resources, and mediates small disagreements. Internally she wrestles with the expectation to follow a traditional career path (her family hopes for a government job) versus her own pull toward a creative life as a visual storyteller. She’s currently navigating the “early‑adult identity” stage: building professional skills while still experimenting with artistic pursuits.

**Core Traits**  
*Observant*: Diya notices subtle details—like the way a friend’s voice softens when they’re uneasy—which makes her a good listener.  
*Resourceful*: She can stretch a single rupee across a week’s meals by buying in bulk and repurposing leftovers.  
*Creative*: Sketching, collage‑making, and writing short poems are daily habits.  
*Grounded*: She prefers practical solutions; when a project stalls, she creates a simple timeline and assigns micro‑tasks.

**Emotional Profile**  
Diya’s default mood is calm, punctuated by bursts of excitement when she discovers a new visual idea or a perfect spice blend. Stress appears as a lingering knot in her shoulders; she counters it with a quick walk along the river or by humming a line from *Mahaprithibi*. Joy is expressed through laughter that often erupts during cooking class, especially when she successfully flips a *paturi* without breaking the leaf. When she feels overlooked, she retreats into her sketchbook, drawing thin black lines that gradually fill the page—a visual metaphor for processing. She rarely shows anger outwardly; instead, she writes a short note to herself, “What’s the real trigger?” before responding.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Diya values authenticity, community, and the ability to weave stories into everyday life. She is motivated by the desire to create a visual diary that captures Kolkata’s rhythm—its trams, riverbanks, and street vendors—so future generations can “see” the city. She fears becoming “just another clerk” in a government office, losing the time to sketch and write. She also worries about disappointing her parents if she chooses a freelance path, and about the loneliness that might follow if she moves to a larger city for work.
