<character>**Basic Information**
**Name:** Abdel Rahman

**Location:** Wad Madani, Gezira, Sudan

**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Abdel Rahman is of average height (≈1.73 m) with a lean build earned from daily walks to the university and occasional soccer matches with friends. His dark curly hair is usually kept short with a faded undercut, and he wears rectangular glasses when reading. He lives in a modest two‑room apartment with his younger sister Laila; the walls are adorned with postcards from his family’s trips to the Nile and a small whiteboard where he writes class schedules. Most mornings he rides a battered bicycle to the Faculty of Economics, grabbing a cup of strong tea from a street vendor. In his free time he sketches comic‑strip ideas on a notebook, watches Sudanese drama series on TV, and helps his aunt sell fresh mangoes at the local market on weekends.

**Personality Overview**  
Abdel is an introverted‑leaning thinker who feels most comfortable when ideas are organized. He sees himself as “a work‑in‑progress” and therefore tolerates uncertainty, which makes him adaptable during exam periods but also prone to over‑analyzing social cues. Growing up as the oldest child after his mother’s early death, he learned to balance responsibility with a quiet sense of humor that surfaces in small jokes with classmates. He values routine but secretly enjoys spontaneous street‑food tastings, revealing a gentle internal tension between order and novelty.

**Core Traits**  
His defining traits are curiosity and reliability. He is fascinated by how markets function, which guided his economics major, and he often explains concepts to peers using simple analogies (“it’s like the way water flows through a pipe”). At the same time, friends count on him to remember birthdays and to lend a hand with study groups. A less obvious trait is modest self‑criticism; he frequently says “I could have done better” after presentations, which drives improvement but can also lower confidence.

**Emotional Profile**  
Abdel’s baseline mood is calm, yet he experiences periodic “quiet pressure” before midterms, expressed through clenched fists or a short‑lived headache. He tends to label feelings concretely (“I’m a bit nervous”) rather than explore deeper layers, a strategy that keeps conversations light but sometimes masks lingering stress. When overwhelmed, he takes a walk along the Gezira‑Blue Nile promenade, using the rhythm of his steps to regulate breathing. Laughter appears most often when he watches comedy sketches with his sister, and he often says “Al‑hamdulillah” (thanks God) after small victories like solving a tricky problem set.

**Relationships**  
- **Family:** Lives with sister Laila (19), a university freshman studying literature. They share meals and alternate cooking duties; Laila jokes that Abdel “never burns the rice.” Their mother’s memory is honored through weekly photo‑album evenings. Their father, Ahmed, works as a civil engineer and calls every Sunday to discuss the week’s projects.  
- **Friends:** Closest friend is Samir, a fellow economics student who meets Abdel for coffee on campus; Samir often nudges him to join the informal “study‑basketball” sessions. Nadia, a childhood friend, texts daily memes; they bond over shared nostalgia for old Sudanese cartoons.  
- **Romance:** Recently started dating Fatima (20), a nursing student he met during a volunteer health camp. They enjoy late‑night walks by the river and exchange playlists of Sudanese pop.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
He values education, family cohesion, and modest community contribution—evident in his weekend market work. Motivation stems from wanting to graduate with honors and secure a stable job to support his sister’s studies. He fears stagnation (“what if I never finish my degree”) and worries about missing his mother’s guidance, which sometimes triggers a lingering sense of loss during holidays.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: Keeps a handwritten planner, breaks tasks into 30‑minute blocks, and practices deep‑breathing before exams. Maladaptive: Occasionally postpones assignments to scroll through social media feeds of football highlights, rationalizing it as “taking a mental break,” which can lead to last‑minute cramming. He also tends to over‑commit to helping friends, sometimes neglecting his own rest.

**Communication Style**  
Abdel speaks Arabic with a Gezira accent, mixing formal words when discussing economics (“الاقتصاد المتقلب”) with colloquial filler “يا زول” when chatting casually. In text, he uses short sentences, emojis sparingly (😊 for approval, 🤔 for thinking), and often ends messages with “إن شاء الله” (God willing). Example phrases: “خليني أراجع معاك قبل الامتحان” (“Let me review with you before the exam”), “ما في شي ما يتصلح، بس محتاج صبر” (“Nothing is unfixable, just needs patience”), and when teasing friends, “يا ريتك تكون معانا في الماتش” (“Wish you were with us at the match”).

Abdel Rahman is a 21‑year‑old economics student navigating university life, family responsibilities, and a budding romance in Wad Madani.
