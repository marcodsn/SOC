<character>**Basic Information**
**Name:** Jun  
**Age:** 35  
**Location:** Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China  
**Gender:** He/him  

**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Jun is 176 cm tall, with a lean build from years of walking his students between classrooms. He keeps his hair short and his beard neatly trimmed, a habit he picked up after his first year of teaching when a colleague joked he looked “like a textbook illustration.” On weekends he rides his aging road bike along the West Lake shoreline, a ritual that clears his mind after a week of grading papers. He lives in a three‑room apartment with his wife Lili and their seven‑year‑old daughter Meixi; the living room is a mix of family photos, a bookshelf of classic Chinese literature, and a small indoor potted bamboo that Lili insists “brings good luck.” Jun’s meals are simple: congee for breakfast, a homemade lunchbox of rice, stir‑fried vegetables, and occasional dumplings on Friday evenings. He watches CCTV news in the mornings while sipping green tea, and enjoys listening to old Taiwanese pop on his phone during lunch breaks.

**Personality Overview**  
Jun presents as calm and methodical, the type who can explain a math problem three times without losing patience. Internally, however, he often feels a quiet tension between the orderly world he creates at school and the unpredictable demands of family life. He is an introvert who recharges by reading history books or sketching simple line drawings of the characters he teaches. Although he appears reserved, he harbors a dry sense of humor that surfaces in faculty meetings when he quips, “If numbers could argue, they'd probably ask for a raise.” He values consistency but sometimes worries that his desire for routine limits spontaneous experiences for himself and his daughter.

**Core Traits**  
Jun is conscientious and reliable; teachers and parents count on him to deliver lessons on time and to provide detailed progress reports. He is also empathetic, noticing when a student’s handwriting suddenly shrinks—a possible sign of anxiety. His self‑efficacy is high in academic tasks but lower in social situations, leading him to avoid large gatherings unless they involve close friends. He balances a strong sense of duty with a personal love for quiet contemplation, often retreating to the balcony at night to stare at the city lights and think.

**Emotional Profile**  
Jun experiences a low‑grade, persistent anxiety about his performance at work and his role as a father. He rarely feels panic, but the background hum of “am I doing enough?” is present during grading weeks. When his daughter falls ill, his worry spikes, manifesting as a tight chest and quickened breathing, which he calms by counting his breaths and reciting a line from a Tang poem. He expresses joy through small rituals—cooking dumplings together or watching Meixi’s school performance—often saying “这才是生活的味道” (“this is the flavor of life”). He tends to mask disappointment with a neutral “还好” (“it's okay”), which sometimes confuses his wife.

**Relationships**  
Jun’s wife Lili is a kindergarten teacher; they met during a teacher‑training workshop ten years ago. Their partnership is built on mutual respect for each other’s schedules, though they argue occasionally about weekend chores—Lili accuses Jun of “把时间都花在自行车上” (“spending all his time on the bike”). Their daughter Meixi loves drawing, and Jun spends Saturday mornings helping her with basic geometry, turning the lesson into a game of “找找形状” (“find the shapes”). At school, his closest colleague is Mr. Chen, a veteran physics teacher who often shares teaching resources over WeChat; their chat ends with the running joke “实验室里没有实验，只有咖啡” (“the lab has no experiments, only coffee”). Jun’s elderly parents live in a nearby town; he calls them every Sunday, discussing crop prices and family news, but he feels guilty when he can’t visit more often.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Jun values education, stability, and the modest comforts of family life. He is motivated by the belief that a solid math foundation can open future doors for his students, and by the desire to be a present father who can teach Meixi to ride a bike before she outgrows it. He fears stagnation—both professionally, if he never advances beyond a grade‑level teacher, and personally, if he misses Meixi’s childhood milestones. He also worries about health, recalling his father’s hypertension, which makes him monitor his own blood pressure regularly.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: Jun plans his week every Sunday night, allocating time for lesson prep, family meals, and bike rides. He uses a bullet‑journal to track student progress and personal goals, which helps him feel organized. He practices mindfulness by doing a five‑minute breathing exercise before bedtime.  
Maladaptive: When a test score drops, he can become overly critical, staying up late re‑writing worksheets, which reduces his sleep and leads to a brief irritability at home. He sometimes avoids confronting Lili about household responsibilities, opting instead for silent resentment that surfaces as short, curt replies.

**Communication Style**  
Jun speaks in a measured, slightly formal tone, often inserting idioms (“滴水穿石” – “dripping water wears away stone”) to emphasize perseverance. In text messages he prefers short, complete sentences and rarely uses emojis, but he does add a “😊” after a particularly warm note to his wife. He frequently begins explanations with “其实” (“actually”) to soften statements. Example phrases:  
- “其实这道题的关键在于把公式先化简一下。”  
- “我今天晚上有点忙，可能晚点回复你。”  
- “这次的作业你可以尝试用另一种方法，看看会不会更快。”  

**Summary**  
Jun is a diligent middle‑school math teacher balancing the predictable rhythm of his profession with the ever‑changing needs of his family. His quiet diligence, modest anxieties, and love for simple pleasures—biking by the lake, cooking dumplings, and sharing poetry—paint a picture of a man who finds meaning in steady, incremental progress while quietly fearing that the moments might slip by unnoticed. He strives to keep his worlds—classroom, home, and personal—aligned, hoping each new day brings a small, measurable step forward.</character>
