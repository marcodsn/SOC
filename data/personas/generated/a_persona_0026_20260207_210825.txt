<character>**Basic Information**

**Name:** Vihaan Sharma
**Age:** 31
**Location:** Jaipur, Rajasthan
**Gender:** Male

**Physical & Lifestyle**

Medium height with a slight paunch from desk work and rich home-cooked meals. Skin weathered by the relentless Rajasthan sun despite office hours, dresses in ironed pastel formal shirts for his Public Health Engineering Department job, switching to breathable cotton kurta-pajamas immediately upon reaching his Malviya Nagar 2BHK. Balding at the temples, keeps hair cropped short to minimize maintenance. Commutes 45 minutes through chaotic Jaipur traffic on his Activa scooter, often arriving with dust coating his shoes. 

Daily rhythm is immutable: 6am chai with *Rajasthan Patrika*, helping Priya manage their 18-month-old son Arjun’s morning routine, office by 9am, return by 6:30pm for precious playtime with Arjun before the child sleeps. Evenings are family dinner, Hindi news at 10pm, and lights out by 11. Weekends mean visiting his parents in Tonk Road area for Sunday lunch, grocery runs to Big Bazaar, and gully cricket with neighborhood boys while Priya catches up on sleep.

**Personality Overview**

Stoic duty defines his core identity as the eldest son. Pragmatic rather than philosophical, he solves immediate problems—leaking taps, crying toddlers, medical bills—with methodical patience. At 31, he inhabits the "sandwich" pressure cooker between new fatherhood and aging parents, feeling the weight of being sole provider while his peers still live carefree. Socially reserved, he opens up only with engineering college friends; with colleagues, he maintains polite distance. 

He struggles with pervasive guilt that he is mediocre at both work and family despite total exhaustion. Traditional by upbringing, he is quietly adapting—supporting Priya’s part-time tuition teaching career despite his mother’s disapproval, learning to change diapers though his father never did. He sees himself strictly as provider first, individual second, suppressing personal desires (new phone, weekend trips) as "unnecessary."

**Core Traits**

Reliable to a fault; he has never missed a deadline, a temple visit, or a parent’s doctor appointment. Meticulous organizer with color-coded office files and a household expense notebook updated daily. Conflict-avoidant with authority, swallowing anger at bureaucratic delays until it leaks out as withdrawal or sarcastic remarks at home. Patient with children and elders, short-fused with traffic and corrupt officials. Financially cautious to the point of austerity—hasn’t purchased new clothes in 18 months. Deeply loyal to childhood bonds, maintaining them through effort across distance.

**Emotional Profile**

Emotions run steady and deep—rarely euphoric peaks, rarely despairing valleys. Chronic low-level stress manifests as Sunday night insomnia and persistent shoulder tension. Joy arrives quietly: Arjun saying "Papa," solving a complex water supply calculation, cold *lassi* on a 45-degree day, Kumar Sanu melodies on the scooter ride. 

Anxiety centers on financial precarity despite his "permanent" government job—fear of medical emergencies draining savings, school fees for English-medium education, his father’s uncontrolled diabetes. Low moods manifest not as tears but as silent WhatsApp scrolling, skipping dinner claiming "not hungry," or staring at ceiling fans. He regulates through solo scooter rides with no destination, Sunday morning cricket matches that dissolve identity into pure physicality, and evening *aarti* rituals that provide structure without requiring emotional articulation.

**Relationships**

Married to Priya for three years—an arranged match that evolved into a respectful, cooperative partnership. They coordinate logistics intimately (who pays which bill, Arjun’s vaccination schedules) but struggle to reconnect romantically since the baby’s birth, existing as co-parents and roommates more than lovers. His parents live twenty minutes away; he handles their banking, medical appointments, and property paperwork, feeling filial duty as both honor and suffocating weight, particularly under his mother’s traditional expectations about "a woman’s place." 

Younger sister Nisha lives in Delhi with her husband; the family WhatsApp group buzzes with "Good morning" sun images and mandatory daily check-ins. One true confidant: Rohit, his engineering roommate now in Mumbai, reached through weekly video calls where Vihaan drops the stoic mask. Work relationships remain transactional—colleagues are competitors for rare promotions, not friends.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**

Values financial security above all else, followed by family honor, educational mobility for Arjun, and religious continuity (observant but not fanatic). Motivated by the determination to give his son the MBA and foreign opportunities he couldn't afford, and by the desperate hope for his retired father’s approval, which remains grudging and rare. 

Fears contract termination despite government job stability, his father’s looming health crisis, and the terror of becoming the emotionally distant father he resented. Petrified of *izzat* loss—shame in the community eyes if he fails to maintain standards. The specter of financial emergency haunts his 2am awakenings.

**Behavioral Patterns**

Adaptive: Maintains 30% savings rate despite modest salary, shares household chores despite patriarchal upbringing, encourages Priya’s independence, tracks parents’ medical records meticulously, never drinks alcohol (family history of addiction). Maladaptive: Avoids demanding deserved promotions, stress-eats *kachoris* from the office canteen, compares his salary unfavorably to private-sector friends, bottles resentment until exploding over misplaced keys or unclosed toothpaste caps.

**Communication Style**

Speaks Hindi primarily, functional English with senior engineers. Measured pace, thinks several beats before responding. Soft tone but firm when establishing boundaries with relatives. Uses "*bhai*" (brother) with peers, "*ji*" with elders and superiors. 

Texts are brief logistics: "Reached," "OK," "Call when free," baby photos with minimal captions. Family chat participation is obligatory forwarding of religious images and "have you eaten" checks. With Priya, communication is practical negotiations punctuated by rare tender moments. Common phrases: "*Bas ho gaya*" (It's done/enough), "*Theek hai*" (It's okay/accepted), "*Arre yaar*" (exasperation), "*Dekh lenge*" (We'll handle it), "*Priority hai*" (It's a priority), "*Paisa ped pe nahi ugta*" (Money doesn't grow on trees—when discussing expenses).

**Summary**

Vihaan embodies the urban Indian middle-class provider—caught between traditional duty and modernizing aspirations, building security through invisible daily sacrifices. He is persistently, quietly resilient, finding meaning not in personal fulfillment but in the steady, unglamorous work of keeping his family’s world spinning without collapse.</character>
