He sleeps on a thin mattress, listens to Bollywood and Sindhi pop on a cheap Bluetooth speaker, and prefers chai with a pinch of cardamom after school.

Bilal is an introverted‑energetic mix: he feels most comfortable in familiar streets but lights up when a new idea pops up during a class discussion. He sees himself as “the kid who fixes things” – whether it’s untangling a headphone cable or solving a math problem his friends can’t crack. He is proud of his modest independence yet worries about living up to his older brother’s academic record. At home he adopts a laid‑back attitude, but at school he can be surprisingly meticulous, especially when preparing for his science fair.

Problem‑solver: Bilal enjoys taking apart old radios to see how they work, then re‑assembling them with duct tape.  
Loyal friend: He rarely misses a cricket match with his mates, even when the heat is intense.  
Quiet humor: He drops a dry one‑liner after a joke, like “That’s as useful as a raincoat in June,” which makes his friends laugh.

Bilal’s emotions surface in short bursts. Success in a lab experiment produces a quick grin and a self‑punch on the arm (“Got it!”). Frustration appears as tapping his foot or sighing loudly while waiting for the bus. He experiences mild anxiety before big exams, which he soothes by drawing doodles of rockets in the margins of his notebook. When a friend is upset, he offers a simple, “What’s up? **Values, Motivations & Fears**
Bilal values practicality, community respect, and the ability to “make things work.” He is motivated by the idea of building a simple gadget that could help his aunt count inventory faster. He fears being labeled “the lazy one” if his grades slip, and worries about missing the chance to study engineering because of financial constraints at home. He also dreads the thought of moving away from Hyderabad and losing his close‑knit circle.
