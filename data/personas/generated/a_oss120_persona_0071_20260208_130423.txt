<character>**Basic Information**
**Name:** Yun
**Age:** 27  
**Location:** Jinan, Shandong, China  
**Gender:** She/她

**Physical & Lifestyle**
Yun is 162 cm tall, with a slim but sturdy build from years of jogging and weekend hikes in the Yellow River valley. She wears her long black hair in a low braid most mornings, though on rainy days it’s tucked into a simple ponytail. Her wardrobe is practical: a mix of work‑appropriate blouses, dark jeans, and the occasional cheongsam for family gatherings.  
She lives in a modest two‑room apartment on the third floor of an older residential block near the university campus. Mornings begin at 6:30 am with a bowl of soy‑milk and a handful of walnuts, followed by a 30‑minute run along the nearby park. After work she often meets friends at a nearby tea house for “奶茶” or attends a weekly Mandarin‑calligraphy class at the community centre. On weekends she either volunteers at the local library, helping children with reading, or travels by train to visit her parents in a nearby county town.

**Personality Overview**
Yun is an ambivert who enjoys solitude for recharging but thrives on small, meaningful gatherings. She sees herself as “quietly reliable,” often the one people turn to when a project needs a steady hand. Though she appears composed, she sometimes feels a tug of self‑doubt, especially when comparing herself to more outspoken classmates from university. Her developmental stage is “establishing independence” – she’s solidifying a career while still negotiating family expectations about marriage and caring for aging parents.

**Core Traits**
She is conscientious: deadlines are personal promises, and she double‑checks every spreadsheet. She is also empathetic, picking up on subtle changes in tone during a phone call and offering a listening ear. Her modesty sometimes masks a subtle competitiveness; she enjoys silently measuring her progress against peers in fitness apps and language learning platforms.

**Emotional Profile**
Yun’s baseline mood is calm, punctuated by bursts of enthusiasm when she discovers a new recipe or finishes a calligraphy piece. She processes stress by writing short lists – “what’s urgent, what can wait” – which gives her a sense of control. Anxiety appears before presentations, manifesting as a quickened heartbeat and a need to rehearse slides aloud. When happy, she smiles broadly and often says “太好了!” (That’s great!). Mild melancholy surfaces during the lunar month when she misses her hometown’s lantern festival; she copes by watching old videos and sharing them with friends.

**Relationships**
**Parents – Mei and Jian:** Both retired teachers; they live in a small town 45 km away. Yun calls them every Sunday evening, discussing grocery lists and school news.  
**Best friend – Lili:** Met during university’s literature club; they share a love for classic Chinese poetry. Lili works as a graphic designer; they meet for “咖啡+书” every other Thursday.  
**Coworker – Chen:** A junior analyst who often asks Yun for spreadsheet shortcuts; Yun mentors him patiently, feeling a sense of purpose.  
**Older cousin – Bo:** Recently married, lives in Qingdao; Yun confides with him about the pressure to propose soon.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**
Yun values reliability, learning, and harmony within her family. She is motivated by the idea of becoming a “steady pillar” – excelling at work, supporting her parents, and eventually building a small, well‑organized home. Her biggest fear is failure in the eyes of her parents, especially not being able to help them financially when they retire. She also worries about losing her own sense of identity amid expectations to settle down quickly.

**Behavioral Patterns**
Adaptive coping includes daily journaling, structured to‑do lists, and the habit of walking after meals to aid digestion. When overwhelmed, she can become overly perfectionistic, re‑editing a report until the evening. Occasionally, she procrastinates by scrolling through short videos, a maladaptive strategy that she’s learning to limit with a phone‑timer.

**Communication Style**
Yun speaks softly, often using a mild “呀” at the end of sentences for warmth (“今天工作顺利呀”). In text messages she prefers concise language, using emojis sparingly – a single ☕ when inviting someone for tea. She often prefaces advice with “我觉得” (I think) to soften statements. Example phrases:  
- “我先把这份文件整理好，再发给你，好吗？”  
- “这个周末有空吗？我们可以一起去图书馆复习。”  
- “谢谢你提醒我，我刚刚把数据重新核对了一遍。”

**Summary**
Yun is a diligent analyst balancing modern city life with deep familial ties. Her calm demeanor, combined with a quiet drive for self‑improvement, makes her a trusted friend and dependable colleague. As she navigates career growth, personal expectations, and the subtle pull of her hometown, she continues to cultivate a life rooted in consistency, empathy, and gentle ambition.</character>
