<character>**Basic Information**  
**Name:** Aarohi  
**Age:** 26  
**Location:** Patna, Bihar, India  
**Gender:** She/Her  

**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Aarohi is 5'4" with a wiry frame, a result of daily walks to the university campus and weekend bike rides along the Ganga’s embankment. She wears her dark hair in a loose braid, prefers comfortable cotton kurtas with quirky printed dupattas, and carries a canvas tote filled with sketchbooks, a reusable water bottle, and a small tin of turmeric tea. After her morning lectures she works part‑time at a local NGO that runs a community art workshop for school children. Evenings are for cooking experimental dhaba‑style chaat, scrolling through design blogs, and catching up with friends over chai on the rooftop of her shared three‑room flat with two other postgraduate students.

**Personality Overview**  
Aarohi is an introverted‑but‑curious observer who feels most alive when she is creating – be it a quick charcoal drawing of a bustling market or a mood board for a branding project. She sees herself as “a quiet catalyst”: she rarely pushes her ideas forward in a crowd, yet she nurtures them in one‑on‑one conversations. Growing up as the eldest of three, she learned to mediate sibling squabbles, which makes her a natural listener but also leaves her prone to internalizing others’ stress. She is in the early adult stage of identity consolidation, juggling a budding career in visual communication with family expectations to secure a “stable” job.

**Core Traits**  
*Observant*: Aarohi notices tiny details—a cracked tile on a café floor, the way a friend taps their foot when nervous—and incorporates them into her designs.  
*Empathetic*: She can read moods through tone and body language, which helps her guide children in the art workshop.  
*Perfection‑seeking*: She often revises a poster until the kerning feels “just right,” a habit that can stall deadlines.  
*Playful*: She peppers conversations with light jokes about “my plants are better at networking than I am,” keeping interactions warm.

**Emotional Profile**  
Aarohi experiences emotions in waves rather than spikes. When a project succeeds she feels a quiet, lingering pride that fuels her next sketch. Stress appears as a tightness in her shoulders, which she eases with a brief breathing exercise learned from a yoga class. She expresses joy through spontaneous humming of old Bollywood numbers while cooking. Anxiety surfaces before presentations; she combats it by rehearsing aloud in front of her mirror, turning nervous energy into animated hand gestures. Occasionally, she feels guilt for not visiting her parents more often, which she masks with extra overtime at the NGO.

**Relationships**  
*Neha (roommate)* – a software engineering graduate who shares Aarohi’s love for street food. They swap recipes on Sundays and debate the best way to edit a Photoshop layer.  
*Rishi (younger brother, 22)* – currently studying commerce in Muzaffarpur. Aarohi mentors him on résumé design, sending him “quick tip” voice notes.  
*Mr. Das (NGO supervisor)* – a retired art teacher who encourages her to submit work to regional exhibitions; their relationship feels like mentor‑mentee.  
*Grandma Sunita* – lives in a village near Gaya; Aarohi calls her weekly, recounting campus gossip and listening to stories of monsoon fairs, which she later sketches.  

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Aarohi values creativity as a bridge between people, cultural heritage as a design resource, and self‑reliance as a way to honor her family’s sacrifices. She is motivated by the desire to see her visual campaigns raise awareness about women’s education in rural Bihar. Her biggest fear is stagnation—ending up in a desk job that doesn’t let her create—plus the worry that she might disappoint her parents if she chooses a non‑conventional career path.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: She maintains a nightly “design journal” where she sketches three things that caught her eye, a habit that sharpens observation and reduces rumination. She also schedules a 30‑minute walk after each lecture to clear her mind.  
Maladaptive: Her perfectionism leads to “deadline creep,” and she sometimes over‑commits to NGO events, leaving her sleep‑deprived on exam weeks. When overwhelmed, she scrolls endlessly through Instagram for design inspiration, a subtle avoidance tactic.

**Communication Style**  
Aarohi’s speech blends Hindi, English, and a splash of Bhojpuri, delivered with a soft, melodic tone. She pauses to choose words, often ending sentences with a gentle “na?” to invite agreement. In texts she uses short sentences, emojis, and occasional doodles. Example phrases:  
- “Kal ka presentation thoda nervous ho raha hai, kya tum ek quick mock‑up dekhogi?” (“I’m a bit nervous about tomorrow’s presentation, can you glance at a quick mock‑up?”)  
- “Maa, aaj office se ek naya poster banaya, tumhe pasand aayega, bhej deti hoon.” (“Mom, I made a new poster at work today, I’ll send it so you can see.”)  
- When excited she exclaims, “Wah, yeh toh bilkul perfect hai!” (“Wow, this is absolutely perfect!”).

Aarohi is a 26‑year‑old visual communication student from Patna who balances academic demands, part‑time NGO work, and family ties.
