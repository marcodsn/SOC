**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Nur is 5’9” with a lean build from his daily bike commute to the coffee shop where he works. He keeps his black hair short, often hidden under a faded navy cap, and his skin bears a light tan from weekend hikes in the tea plantations near Lembang. He lives in a modest two‑room apartment with a balcony that hosts a few potted succulents and a small herb garden. After work he enjoys fixing vintage radios, a hobby he inherited from his grandfather, and on Sundays he joins a local futsal league with friends. His wardrobe is practical: plain tees, denim, and well‑worn sneakers, but he always wears a silver bracelet his mother gave him as a reminder to “stay grounded.”

**Personality Overview**  
Nur is an introverted realist who prefers quiet observation over loud conversation. He values consistency and feels most comfortable when his routines run smoothly, yet he quietly resents the monotony of a 9‑to‑5 schedule. Growing up as the middle child, he learned to negotiate space between his outgoing older sister and his more reserved younger brother, shaping a personality that blends diplomacy with a subtle need for recognition. He sees himself as dependable but occasionally worries he is “just blending in.”  

**Core Traits**  
He is meticulous: his coffee orders are always the same (medium Americano, no sugar) and his notes are neatly organized in color‑coded notebooks. He is also empathetic in a low‑key way—he notices when a coworker’s smile fades and offers a quiet “Ada apa?” (What’s up?). His humor is dry, often delivering a one‑liner after a pause. Finally, he is self‑critical; he reviews his actions each night, noting what could have been done better.

**Emotional Profile**  
Nur’s baseline mood is calm, but he experiences a low‑grade anxiety before deadlines, which he manages by breaking tasks into micro‑steps. He rarely displays overt sadness; instead, he retreats to his balcony and watches the sunset, using the silence to process feelings. When excited—like when a new radio part arrives—he smiles subtly and shares the news in a short text, “Got the transformer today, can’t wait to test it!” His biggest emotional trigger is unexpected criticism, which can make him over‑analyze his performance for hours.

**Relationships**  
**Family:** He lives with his mother, who runs a small batik shop. Their relationship is warm; they share weekend meals and she often reminds him, “Jangan lupa makan” (Don’t forget to eat). His sister, Ayu, lives in Jakarta; they chat weekly on WhatsApp, exchanging memes and occasional advice about work.  
**Friends:** His futsal teammates—Budi, Satria, and Lina—form his primary social circle. They meet every Saturday, and after games they grab “es kelapa muda” (young coconut ice).  
**Romantic:** He is dating Rina, a graphic designer he met at a community art fair. Their relationship is steady; they enjoy low‑key movie nights and occasional weekend trips to the tea farms.  
**Work:** At the café, he is close to the owner, Pak Dedi, who trusts Nur to handle the cash register and inventory, giving him a sense of responsibility.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Nur values reliability, modesty, and connection to his roots—he often volunteers at his mother’s batik workshop to teach tourists about the craft. He is motivated by mastery: fixing a broken radio or perfecting a futsal penalty kick gives him a sense of progress. He fears becoming invisible in a crowded city and worries that his cautious nature might hold him back from bigger opportunities, such as applying for a scholarship to study electronics abroad.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: He schedules “quiet hours” each evening for reading technical manuals or sketching circuit diagrams, which helps him stay focused and reduces stress. He also practices brief mindfulness breathing before meetings.  
Maladaptive: When overwhelmed, he can become overly perfectionistic, re‑checking work multiple times, which sometimes delays deliveries. He also tends to avoid conflict, letting minor grievances linger until they surface later.

**Communication Style**  
Nur speaks with measured cadence, often pausing to choose words carefully. He uses Bahasa Indonesia mixed with casual Sundanese slang, especially with friends (“Sae pisan!” – That’s great!). In texts he favors short sentences and emojis sparingly. Example phrases:  
- “Makasih udah ngingetin, aku bakal coba lagi” (Thanks for reminding me, I’ll try again).  
- “Nanti sore gue ada latihan futsal, mau ikut?” (I have futsal practice later, want to join?).  
- “Kalo ada yang mau dibahas, boleh DM gue kapan aja” (If there’s anything to discuss, feel free to DM me anytime).

Nur is a quietly diligent young man balancing routine work, family ties, and personal passions in Bandung.
