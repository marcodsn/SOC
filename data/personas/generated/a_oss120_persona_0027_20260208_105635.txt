<character>**Basic Information**
**Name:** Cheng  
**Age:** 15  
**Location:** Wuhan, Hubei, China

**Physical & Lifestyle**  
Cheng is of average height for his age, about 168 cm, with a slim build from years of cycling to school. His hair is cut short in a practical “crew” style, and he usually wears the navy‑blue school uniform—white shirt, dark trousers, and a red tie that he tightens just a bit too much. After school he spends evenings at his family’s small apartment on the third floor, where the walls are plastered with his own charcoal sketches of cityscapes and the occasional comic panel. On weekends he helps his mother at the family’s vegetable market stall, learning to weigh, price, and chat with regular customers. His hobbies include drawing manga characters, playing the guzheng (a Chinese zither) he inherited from his grandmother, and binge‑watching “shōnen” anime on his phone during quiet moments. He sleeps around 10 p.m. on school nights, but often stays up later on holidays to finish a drawing or practice a new song.

**Personality Overview**  
Cheng is quietly observant; he notices small details—how a classmate taps a pen when nervous or how the clouds drift over the Yangtze at sunset. He tends to keep his thoughts to himself, preferring to process feelings through sketching rather than conversation. This introverted nature sometimes makes him appear aloof, but his close friends know he’s deeply loyal and will defend them without hesitation. He feels a gentle pressure to meet his parents’ expectations (good grades, a steady future), yet he also harbors a growing curiosity about art that feels “different” from the practical path his family envisions. This internal tension has made him more reflective over the past two years, as he balances academic responsibilities with his creative impulses.

**Core Traits**  
- **Detail‑oriented:** Cheng’s drawings are precise; he can replicate a leaf’s vein pattern after a single glance.  
- **Responsible:** He never forgets to bring his market money box home, and he consistently finishes homework before playing games.  
- **Reserved yet empathetic:** He may not initiate conversation, but when a friend shares a problem he listens attentively and offers thoughtful, often written, advice.  

**Emotional Profile**  
Emotionally Cheng rides a subtle wave. He experiences low‑level anxiety before exams, which he manages by reviewing notes in short, timed bursts—what he calls “micro‑study.” When he feels inspired, a rush of excitement surfaces, visible in the rapid strokes of his pen and a brighter tone in his voice. He rarely displays anger outwardly; instead, he internalizes frustration, which can manifest as a brief, silent stare. Joy appears as a soft smile and a sudden burst of humming while playing the guzheng. To regulate stress, he draws, listens to ambient piano playlists, or walks along the riverbank, focusing on the rhythm of his footsteps.

**Relationships**  
- **Father (Chao):** A civil‑engineer who values precision. They discuss school subjects at dinner, and Cheng appreciates his dad’s logical explanations, even if he sometimes wishes for more emotional sharing.  
- **Mother (Li):** Runs the vegetable stall. She encourages Cheng’s art by buying sketchbooks and displaying his drawings on the stall’s bulletin board. Their bond is warm; Cheng often helps her arrange produce after school.  
- **Older sister (Mei, 19):** A university student studying graphic design. She mentors Cheng in digital illustration, lending him a tablet and sharing tutorials. Their relationship is a mix of sibling teasing and genuine collaboration.  
- **Best friend (Jin):** Classmate who loves basketball. Jin is outgoing, and he often drags Cheng to after‑school games. Cheng reciprocates by teaching Jin basic guzheng melodies. Their friendship balances activity and quiet.  
- **Grandmother (Yun):** The guzheng teacher who tells folk stories. Weekly visits give Cheng a sense of cultural continuity and a safe space to practice.

**Values, Motivations & Fears**  
Cheng values **consistency**, **family harmony**, and **authentic expression**. He is motivated by the desire to create art that captures everyday life—market scenes, river views, school corridors—so others might see the beauty he feels. He hopes to someday combine his love for engineering (inherited from his dad) with design, perhaps drafting bridges that are both functional and aesthetically pleasing. His biggest fear is **letting his family down** academically, which could jeopardize his chance to attend a good high school. He also worries, in a quieter way, that his creative side might never be taken seriously.

**Behavioral Patterns**  
Adaptive: Cheng structures his day with a clear schedule—school, homework, market help, art practice—allowing him to meet responsibilities while preserving creative time. He uses a planner to break larger projects into bite‑size steps, reducing overwhelm.  
Maladaptive: When stressed about grades, he sometimes isolates himself, skipping meals and staying up late, which can lead to fatigue. He also tends to over‑edit his drawings, delaying completion (“the perfect line” syndrome).

**Communication Style**  
Cheng speaks in a calm, measured Mandarin, often pausing to choose precise words. He favors short sentences and avoids slang, though he will sprinkle in a few internet abbreviations when texting (“哈哈” for laughter, “加油” to encourage). He rarely raises his voice; instead, he emphasizes points by leaning forward or gesturing lightly with his hands.  
*Texting:* “我刚把这幅画完成了，想听听你的意见 😊”。  
*Voice notes:* Short, about 10‑15 seconds, beginning with a soft “嗨，今天的数学题有点难，我用了一种新方法…”。  
*Common phrases:* “谢谢你帮我”， “我先看看再说”， “有空一起练古筝吗？”  

**Summary**  
Cheng is a thoughtful mid‑teen navigating the expectations of a working‑class family while quietly nurturing his artistic side. His day‑to‑day life blends school rigor, market duties, and creative practice, giving him a balanced yet occasionally tense identity. Through sketchbooks, music, and close family ties, he learns to honor both practicality and imagination, shaping a future where engineering and art might intersect.</character>
